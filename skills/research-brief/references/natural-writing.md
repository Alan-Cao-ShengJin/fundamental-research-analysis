# Edit research prose for the reader

Apply after the analysis and figures are settled, before rendering a report or delivering a research answer. Use the user's voice sample when supplied; otherwise write as a concise professional analyst addressing an informed investor. Return the edited result, without publishing drafts or an editorial critique unless requested.

This guide adapts the editing principles in [Humanizer v3.1.0](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/0ef9fe5bc3572734fd3ae6c448fe00a30cd5dcf0/SKILL.md), Copyright (c) 2025 Siqi Chen, MIT licensed. It is integrated into this skill; no separate installation is required.

## Edit meaning and structure

- Write the conclusion directly, then give its reason. Name the actor and action: "Management cut the store target" rather than "The trajectory reflects a moderation in expansion ambition." Use a short causal explanation, not an abstract phrase that makes the reader reconstruct the argument.
- Prefer "Profit can grow 10% because...", "The stock is too expensive because..." and "Governance risk justifies the discount because..." to phrases such as ordinary delivery, recognition, cash access or return path without a concrete explanation. Keep a qualifier only when it changes the investment meaning.
- For a stock pitch, state the investment judgment in ordinary language before accounting qualifications. Explain why the price compensates, or fails to compensate, for business and governance risks. Do not turn the opening into a technical status report or hedge every clause.
- Check each paragraph's reader path: claim, evidence, explanation and investment consequence. Supply missing links instead of assuming the reader can reconstruct the model.
- Round headline multiples naturally and explain the exact valuation nearby. Define the fiscal period and profit basis; distinguish earnings, adjusted earnings and cash after investment. Clear writing cannot repair an incorrectly advanced earnings year.
- State the decision and economic reason first. Replace abstract praise with a specific change in sales, costs, cash, shareholder rights or value.
- Remove introductory announcements, invented objections, rhetorical question-and-answer openings, dramatic endings and sentences that repeat the heading or preceding paragraph.
- Give each material fact one main home. Use a caption to explain a chart's implication; do not narrate every plotted value again. Repeating the call/target in a brief and its separate main report is useful; repeating the same argument throughout one report is not.
- Replace generic authority with the named source and its actual claim. Distinguish what management said from what the model assumes.
- Use direct verbs and natural sentence lengths. Cut formulaic contrasts, lists padded to three items, unnecessary dashes, strings of qualifiers and inflated adjectives. Keep a comparison when it answers a real investment question.
- Use headings that name the subject. Reserve bold for the decision, key figures and a few phrases needed for scanning. Use lists for genuinely parallel information.

## Profit labels and valuation language

Use **net profit attributable to shareholders** for the listed company's earnings after excluding non-controlling interests. After defining it, **attributable net profit** is a concise label. Do not call it parent profit, which is unclear to investors and can be confused with the parent company's standalone accounts. Do not substitute group profit, operating profit, adjusted profit or cash flow. Internal metric IDs can remain stable if their definitions are correct; update the displayed label.

Introduce a valuation claim where it contributes to the argument: "The shares trade at about 5x FY2027 earnings." Follow it with a short calculation, such as forecast attributable net profit divided by shares to obtain EPS, then price divided by EPS to obtain P/E. A heading such as **Valuation** is enough. Do not add a heading like "Where about 5x earnings comes from" when no such claim has been introduced. The number need not appear in the main headline if another point is more important.

Explain **rerating** on first use as investors paying a higher P/E for the company's earnings. Then state why they would do so. "The price needs a rerating" is an admission of a dependency, not an investment argument. Replace "unchanged multiple" with **if investors keep paying today's P/E** and show what that means for the target price and return. With the same forecast EPS at both ends, no higher P/E means no price gain; with a different earnings forecast, calculate the earnings-driven change and explain the period.

Use the [return hurdle](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md#return-hurdle-and-calls) when describing attractiveness. A 20% base-case price upside is Buy by rating, but unattractive under this mandate's 50% hurdle. Say both directly. Do not confuse the Buy label with an attractive capital-allocation opportunity; a large bull-case return cannot repair a weak base-case payoff.

Do not invent personality, firsthand experience, anecdotes or certainty to make the writing sound human. Financial research should sound clear and candid. Punctuation and individual words are editing signals, not proof of authorship; a mechanical blacklist can damage precise terminology.

## Preserve the investment meaning

Protect the call, reference price/time, target/date, scenario prices, signs, units, currencies, reporting scope, citations and actual/forecast boundary. Preserve the rating thresholds and assess the 50% attractiveness hurdle separately; a sub-50% Buy is not automatically Hold. Preserve distinctions between price and total return, profit and cash, statutory and adjusted earnings, group profit and profit attributable to shareholders, and attainable target versus intrinsic value.

Remove repeated hedges while retaining uncertainty that changes the decision. A forecast remains conditional. A governance allegation does not become a finding. An unavailable series does not become evidence of absence. A shorter sentence must not imply a causal relationship that the source did not establish.

A prose-only edit must not change model inputs, formulas, data, code, identifiers or link targets. If editing exposes a factual problem, resolve it through the evidence/model workflow and regenerate affected outputs.

## Final pass

Read the report as an investor deciding whether the call is credible. Can the reader immediately say what the company earns, why the price is attractive or unattractive, what would change the valuation and what happens if it does not? Rewrite any passage that depends on unexplained shorthand. Remove sentences that add no fact, necessary inference, assumption or investment consequence. Move supporting detail to the appendix; retain material risks in the pitch. Check figures, sources and scenarios against the current decision record. Deliver only the final prose.
