# Institutional research writing

Write for a portfolio manager or investment analyst. Use a formal, measured and concise research voice, with precise financial terminology and explicit attribution. This standard governs the brief and full report.

Apply the [selective brief guide](one-page-brief.md), preserving substantive qualifications and source references. Professional tone does not require ornate vocabulary, passive constructions or longer sentences. Prefer the shortest wording that states the economic mechanism accurately.

## Argument sequence

Use this sequence for each substantive investment point in briefs, full reports and update notes:

1. **State the point and data.** Lead with the investment-relevant claim and the specific observation or estimate supporting it. Identify the period and metric basis where they affect interpretation.
2. **Explain how the data was derived.** For reported data, identify the disclosure and any calculation or adjustment. For forecasts, expose the principal business drivers, evidence and assumptions that produce the number. For valuation, explain the economic or comparable basis of the selected input as well as the arithmetic. A definition, citation alone, "our model" or "analyst judgment" does not supply the missing derivation.
3. **Explain its significance.** Interpret the result against a relevant comparison or economic requirement. Explain the mechanism and materiality for earnings, cash generation, value, shareholder access or risk. State why the difference matters rather than leaving the reader to infer it.
4. **Tie it back to the stock view.** Explain how that consequence supports, weakens or changes the investment case at the stated price. Separate better business performance from an attractive stock payoff; retain the strongest contrary explanation and material uncertainty.

Write this as a natural argument in concise, connected prose. Do not print four mechanical labels, force exactly four sentences, or append a recommendation slogan after every figure. Related evidence can share one derivation and implication. Put detailed calculations in linked support, but keep the assumptions and causal bridge needed to judge the main point on the page.

Synthetic illustration, with invented units and assumptions:

> Planned distributions exceed forecast free cash flow by 0.6bn. The illustrative model assumes 1.8bn of operating cash flow less 0.5bn of capital expenditure, producing 1.3bn available against 1.9bn of dividends and repurchases. The gap requires cash reserves or financing and reduces capacity to absorb an operating shortfall. This weakens the distribution-led investment case and makes funding a reason for caution in the stock view.

Before finalising, trace each main argument through all four steps. Where derivation is missing, resolve it through evidence/model/valuation work or narrow the affected claim with a concise material qualification. Do not invent support or turn the brief into a running commentary on the research process. Preserve selective useful writing: each argument must earn its place.

Keep the strength of an inference within the evidence. Rising aggregate market share supports a competitive-position argument; it does not establish that every customer was retained, prices held or costs were passed through. A permanent tax or regulatory cost can be economically offset, but that does not make the policy temporary. Distinguish a lower earnings level from damage to earnings durability before selecting a valuation response. Headlines must preserve those distinctions.

## Editorial standard

Use **we forecast**, **we estimate** and **we assume** to own the analysis. Replace constructions such as "the build assumes" or "the model believes" with the analyst judgment and its economic basis. In investor prose, write "consensus" or "the consensus estimate" rather than "the provider's consensus"; keep the named data source in citations or support. Removing procedural wording must not hide material earnings-definition or estimate-vintage limitations.

Make compressed technical language understandable on first reading. Translate a cohort into the comparable group of stores, customers or contracts being discussed; explain what costs a cash measure includes and why a valuation input matters. A derived P/E restates a cash valuation and is not independent evidence for it.

- Use the argument sequence above. Explain unfamiliar terms within the reasoning; definitions and comparisons support the derivation and interpretation rather than replacing them. Match periods, currencies, earnings definitions and entity scope. Compare EPS with the issuer's own history/guidance or relate it to the share price; raw EPS levels across different companies are not a valuation benchmark. Do not invent a peer benchmark or describe management guidance as consensus.
- Select before polishing. Keep a point only if it changes the investment assessment or supplies evidence needed to judge it. Omit routine checklist findings and familiar background; combine overlapping arguments. Preserve material contrary evidence and forecast-critical qualifications.
- Lead with the investment conclusion and its economic basis. Develop the argument through evidence, forecasts, valuation and the strongest counter-case.
- Use normal analyst language: "we forecast," "we estimate," "management guides," "operating leverage," "cash reserves," "capital expenditure" and "valuation multiple." Attribute the judgment rather than implying certainty.
- Explain a causal mechanism precisely. Identify the revenue driver, expense, investment requirement or shareholder claim affected. Keep business terminology instead of replacing it with conversational imagery.
- Distinguish reported results, management guidance, consensus estimates and our forecasts. Use "the reference price implies" only with a stated calculation; do not present a unique market belief as observable fact.
- Avoid colloquialisms, slogans, dramatic contrasts, rhetorical questions and promotional claims. Avoid both informal shorthand and unnecessary technical abstraction. Use headings and emphasis for navigation, not persuasion by repetition.
- Retain uncertainty where it affects the decision. A valuation multiple remains a judgment, a forecast remains conditional and a scoped governance review does not establish the absence of misconduct.

Examples of the intended register:

| Avoid | Prefer, when supported by the analysis |
| --- | --- |
| Little room for costs to disappoint | The valuation is sensitive to the pace of margin recovery. |
| What investors will pay | The valuation multiple investors assign to forecast earnings. |
| Buybacks use the cash buffer | Share repurchases reduce cash reserves. |
| Cheaper rivals | Lower-priced competitors. |
| Cash left over | Free cash flow after capital expenditure, if that is the metric intended. |
| The difference rests chiefly on costs | The forecast divergence primarily reflects cost and savings assumptions. |

These examples guide register; they are not a mechanical replacement dictionary. Do not substitute a more technical phrase if it changes the metric or weakens the explanation. A simple word is appropriate when it is precise.

## Profit labels and valuation language

Use **net profit attributable to shareholders** for the listed company's earnings after excluding non-controlling interests. After defining it, **attributable net profit** is a concise label. Do not call it parent profit or substitute group profit, operating profit, adjusted earnings or cash flow. Internal identifiers can remain stable when their displayed definitions are correct.

Introduce a valuation assertion with its period and earnings basis: "The shares trade at approximately 25x forecast FY2028 core EPS." Explain the calculation nearby and justify the multiple. Preserve reasonable headline rounding, with exact supporting calculations available. Apply the [target and earnings-basis contract](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md#target-and-earnings-basis).

For a multiple-based target, keep the dated benchmark, earnings window, reason for the selected level and observable repricing mechanism in the argument. Distinguish a provider-reported mean from a calculated history, and a market observation from fair value. A target-price multiplication or a statement that the multiple changes does not supply the investment reasoning.

Use **multiple expansion** or **multiple contraction** when that is the return driver. Explain the operating evidence, allocation change or risk reassessment that supports it. Show the alternative outcome if the current multiple persists. A reference to sentiment or investor confidence alone does not justify a selected multiple.

Distinguish adjusted earnings from cash after investment, price return from dividend yield, economic value from a realisable target, and business quality from investment attractiveness. Apply the [decision contract](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md).

## Editing workflow

1. Establish the current numerical decision, model outputs, evidence and qualifications before drafting.
2. Draft each main point through data, derivation, significance and stock-view implication in the professional register above. Check the causal connection before polishing the wording.
3. Edit for precision, concision and attribution. Remove repetition and informal phrases without stripping out the industry or financial language that carries the analysis.
4. Compare the final text with the numerical and evidence records. Preserve prices, dates, signs, units, forecast periods, source links, uncertainty and the strongest counter-case. A wording revision does not authorize changing forecasts or the call.
5. Render the document and inspect the final reading order, financial labels and page fit. Shorten low-value prose rather than reducing type size or deleting a material qualification. Deliver the final prose, without an editorial transcript or an AI-detector score.

Presentation-facing text in a generator may be revised to implement this edit. Protect numerical assumptions, formula logic, identifiers and link targets. If a factual problem is discovered, resolve it through the evidence/model workflow before regenerating dependent outputs.
