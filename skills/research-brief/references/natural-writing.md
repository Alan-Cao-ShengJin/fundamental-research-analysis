# Research writing

This is the common standard for the writer and the reviewer. The report exists to make the reader understand what we found, in as few words as that takes. Aim for sharp, concise and easy to read: the point first, plain words, short sentences. Stiff phrasing fails because the reader has to decode it; padding fails because the reader has to wade through it. Professional means accurate, specific and honest about what we do not know; it does not mean formal. Keep the economic drivers ahead of the detailed valuation calculation. The decision contract governs the call; style must never change the underlying forecast or hide contrary evidence.

## Argument sequence

For a full initiation, the compact opening can show the call and target. Develop business/industry drivers and their earnings/cash consequences before multi-page band analysis, peer valuation or the detailed target calculation. A short early statement of what investors debate is useful; it should not become the detailed valuation section before the business is explained. Focused valuation-only requests can stay narrow.

Separate cash yield from capital gain when explaining the payoff. “The return is the yield” requires support from the price scenario; a substantial modeled price decline can offset the cash. Combined illustrations need the same horizon and eligible receipts, with gross/net assumptions stated; do not add dividends into the house rating or target. Prefer a useful explanation over another mandatory table. Quantified automatic triggers must remain true after display rounding; say “reassess around” for approximate watch levels.

For each material conclusion, connect the point and data, its derivation, its economic significance and its implication for the investment view. Related paragraphs may share that chain. The derivation is the reasoning and the two or three figures that carry it; the arithmetic stays in the cited exhibit. A citation establishes traceability; test the inference against a plausible competing explanation. State material assumptions where the reader needs them. A completed Argument block does not establish support.

Give each developed argument one main home. The front page and standalone brief summarize it; elsewhere refer back in a clause instead of re-proving the target or repeating the same facts. An implication may concern margin, cash or risk without repeating the recommendation. A debate's quantified effect belongs with its answer, not in every later paragraph.

## Editorial standard

- State the thesis in one or two sentences a reader could repeat to a colleague, and open with it; the sections that follow support or test it.
- Be brief. Every sentence makes a point the reader needs; cut the words, sentences and paragraphs that do not. Plain does not mean longer.
- Use the words you would say aloud. One idea to a sentence; when a semicolon brings in a third idea, split the sentence. No slogans.
- Write who does what: "the board keeps the cash", not "capital is not distributed".
- Say in plain words how each business earns its profit, and why a number moved, before giving the figures. A paragraph that only lists figures has not made its point.
- Carry two or three figures in a sentence, and say what a figure means: "nearly a fifth of profit is bank interest", not "finance income is 18% of profit before tax". A run of figures belongs in the exhibit.
- Use a technical term where it is the exact name of the thing, and explain it in a clause the first time the argument depends on it.
- Give the business reason for an assumption, not the spreadsheet step: never "the build applies" or "the model sets".
- When a conclusion is not obvious, give the steps that lead to it, one short sentence each.
- Do not write to protect yourself. Keep a qualification only if it would change what the reader does, state it once, and say what we do not know as directly as what we do.
- Own judgments with "we estimate", "we forecast" or "we assume". Distinguish reported data, guidance, consensus and our interpretation. Do not invent a variant view, consensus composition or unique market belief.
- Give figures their period, unit, accounting/share basis and relevant comparison. Use model-bound values for material derived claims. Different valid bases may coexist when each is labeled and reconciled.
- Explain the economic meaning of a statistical result in plain words; keep supporting dispersion statistics and window alternatives in the exhibit unless they are needed to judge the conclusion. Do not replace a qualified result with an unsupported claim of a historical extreme.

Keep the claim within the evidence: a company mix change is not a reported industry demand shift; an illustrative bridge to consensus is not what analysts necessarily assume; selected comparisons are not every possible comparison; cash that covers a shortfall for years is not a dividend that cannot be cut. Use computed counts and the exact tested scope for numerical generalisations. A reviewer correction follows the same source, calculation and rounding standard as an author figure.
- Select the evidence that changes earnings, cash, value or risk. Preserve the strongest counter-case and decisive qualifications. Omit routine background and procedural narration; do not bury a headline limitation in an appendix.
- Explain why the selected valuation is appropriate and state it once, with its anchor. Alternatives that change the rating go in one exhibit; do not explain house conventions or defend the method against objections the reader has not raised. Similar answers from dependent methods do not independently validate it.
- Use headings, restrained color and emphasis for navigation. Tables and charts must earn their space; a compact table can communicate a small earnings bridge better than a chart.

## Before and after

Invented companies and figures. Each "before" is accurate and still fails: the reader has to decode it or wade through it. Each "after" is plainer and shorter, and uses only facts already in the "before": a writer who needs a fact the analysis does not hold sends the point back to analysis and does not invent it. Figures taken out of a sentence stay in the cited exhibit.

**Lead with the idea, not the data.**

> Before: Revenue grew 24% in FY2025 to 4.1bn, gross margin widened 1.2 points to 38.4%, and capacity is guided to reach 60,000 tonnes in 2027 against 32,000 today; the broker component forecasts assume the plant opens in 2027, while we defer it to 2028 and forecast FY2027E EPS of 2.10 against consensus of 2.45.
>
> After: Brokers expect the new plant to nearly double capacity in 2027. We think it opens a year late, which puts 2027 profit about 14% below what analysts expect.

**Say how the business earns its profit before measuring it.**

> Before: Grocery distribution to independent retailers was 88% of FY2025 revenue of 9.2bn but 6% of operating profit of 610m; supplier shelf-space rebates of 340m and rental income of 235m on owned warehouses carried margins of 100% and 74%.
>
> After: The company delivers groceries to small shops but makes very little on delivery. Its profit comes from brands paying for shelf space and from rent on the warehouses it owns (Exhibit 2).

**Say what the figures mean; leave the run of figures to the exhibit.**

> Before: From end-March 2025 (close 41.2, forward EPS 2.75) to end-September 2026 (52.6, 3.48), the price rose 27.7% and forward EPS 26.5%, so the forward P/E moved from 15.0x to 15.1x (range 14.0x-16.2x).
>
> After: The shares are up about a quarter in eighteen months, and so is expected profit. Investors have paid 14 to 16 times that profit throughout (Exhibit 5): the shares rose because profit did, not because the valuation did.

**Write who does what.**

> Before: Governance supports the distribution of profit (100% payout) but not of capital (no return to date), and the 65% controlling shareholding constrains minority access to the cash balance.
>
> After: The company pays out all it earns but has never returned the cash it already holds. The parent owns 65% and decides; other shareholders cannot force a payout.

**Give the steps, one short sentence each.**

> Before: Unless distributed, 1.00 of retained cash is worth about 0.40 to a holder, since its 3% deposit return is distributed and capitalised at the 7.5% required yield.
>
> After: A dollar kept in the bank earns about 3 cents a year, which is paid out. An investor wanting 7.5% pays 40 cents for 3 cents a year. So a dollar inside the company is worth 40 cents to a shareholder until the board hands it over.

**Give the business reason, not the spreadsheet step.**

> Before: The build applies a 3.4% return to 2H26 and 3.3% to 2027-28 on the cash balance after dividends, reflecting the one- to two-year deposit ladder.
>
> After: Interest income should fall only slowly: most cash sits in one- to two-year deposits, so a rate cut bites only as each matures. We assume the return slips from 3.4% to 3.3%.

**State the valuation once, with its reason; do not defend the method.**

> Before: The target capitalises FY2027E DPS of 0.60 at 7.5%, the mean FY2027E yield of the state-controlled utilities that the same income funds hold (Exhibit 12). It uses FY2027E, although the house default would still use FY2026E; the choice moves the target by 0.07 and leaves the rating unchanged, and the selection of the comparable set is a judgment.
>
> After: People own this stock for its dividend. The state-controlled utilities held by the same income funds yield 7.5%. On our 2027 dividend of 0.60 that gives a target of 8.00.

**When two facts pull apart, explain why and say which reading the evidence favours.**

> Before: Volume rose 14% in 1H26 while profit fell 4%. Unit profit was 4.95 in 1H25, 4.11 in 2H25 and 4.16 in 1H26; the decline was tested against customer mix (existing customers 4.98 to 4.18, Exhibit 4) and is attributed to input costs ahead of pricing, with the build restoring 4.90 by 2H27E.
>
> After: Volume rose 14% but profit fell 4%. Either new customers pay less, which lasts, or raw materials rose before prices could follow, which reverses. Profit per unit fell as much on existing customers (Exhibit 4), so we think it is cost and expect the margin back by late 2027.

**Do not write to protect yourself.**

> Before: These are priced scenarios, not probabilities or loss limits, and the target should be read as an anchor that cannot be distinguished from the price, not as evidence of mispricing; with surplus cash distributed the target would be about 15% higher.
>
> After: Our target is 1% above the price: the shares are fairly priced. A payout from the cash pile would change that, adding about 15% to our target.

**Cut padding.**

> Before: It is worth noting that, looking at the company's results in each year since 2022, what stands out is that margins have been moving steadily higher, which we think is an important point for investors to bear in mind.
>
> After: Margins have risen every year since 2022.

## Profit labels and valuation language

Use net profit attributable to shareholders for earnings after minorities; define any recurring, adjusted or cash measure. Distinguish operating performance, EPS, cash after required investment/obligations and cash accessible to common shareholders. Keep price return separate from dividends. Preserve the selected current-forward earnings period under the [decision contract](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md).

## Editing workflow

Draft the argument, then edit once for reasoning and once against the pairs above. Read it as someone who has never seen the company: rewrite any sentence that reader would have to stop and work out, and cut any they would not miss. Resolve factual defects through the owning analysis rather than polishing them away. A concise summary may omit peripheral detail; it must not turn a conditional conclusion into an unconditional one. Write nothing we cannot support: every fact traces to a filing, a data series, an attributed comment or the model. Before release, reread every sentence of the report and brief against the evidence and delete or qualify what does not hold. Review and release follow the [publication workflow](../../report-layout/references/publication-review.md).
