# One-page investment brief

Write a selective investment argument on one readable A4 page. The brief helps an investor make a decision; it is not a condensed inventory of every research task.

Every section is about the company: economics, forecasts, valuation and risks. Do not write about missing work, data gaps, where the data came from, internal rules or what is not a catalyst. The headline reads like a sell-side note title: the central investment thesis in one short, plain line of business language, consistent with the call, that a reader understands without the text below; describe a pricing error only where the expectations and valuation analysis supports one. Ordinary words: "Higher Input Costs Cut Margins, but Volumes Keep Growing", "New Capacity Should Lift Sales From 2027" or "Priced for a Shrinking Core Brand While Group Profit Still Grows". No wordplay, slogans or fill-in templates. The call and price return sit beside it, so do not repeat them. State each figure once, rounded as a PM would say it. State uncertainty as a business risk with its effect on value. Finish essential work before writing the brief.

## Select the content

Always make the call and economic rationale understandable, reproduce the primary valuation, and retain the strongest counter-case and evidence that would change the view. Preserve the [decision contract](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md): security, reference price/date, 12-month target, signed price return and same-horizon scenario outcomes. Dividend yield stays separate from price return. Put one primary target in the header; develop the economic drivers before the narrative valuation calculation. Explain its anchor and any comparable consensus difference; keep bear and bull cases with little emphasis. Include the last results, next-period expectations and any upcoming catalyst investors should be prepared for.

A reading order that works for a single-stock brief: what the last results showed and why the stock moved; the competitive evidence behind the call; our forecast, then consensus and why we differ, as separate paragraphs; valuation, starting from our forward P/E against the historical or peer anchor, with the consensus P/E in brackets, two fiscal years of P/E and the re-rating upside to the target; and what to watch, naming the next dated result, the metrics that test the thesis and what would make the de-rating structural, with the bear case. Give every month its year (April 2027, not April).

Choose narrative headings around the argument. The blank payload suggests investment case, valuation and key risks; these are starting points, not required labels or a fixed section count. Combine related reasoning. A paragraph earns its space through its effect on earnings, cash, value, risk or conviction.

Include business background, competitive position, management, ownership, events and financial tables only when they help judge the case. Routine board-role summaries, generic market descriptions and tentative dates need no standalone sections. A table must support the same argument sequence as prose: point/data, derivation, significance and connection to the stock view. Its caption or nearby prose must supply the reasoning that its cells cannot. Define unfamiliar metrics, their units and period; show a comparable baseline, guidance, peer, valuation basis or funding requirement. A list of forecast values alone is insufficient. Prefer concise contextual prose when a table would repeat it; omit the table rather than add data for completeness. A forecast-critical period or adjusted-earnings qualification remains beside the affected figures.

Selectivity does not justify omitting an important risk, binding exclusion, inconvenient cross-check or substantive uncertainty. Keep the main investment reasoning on the page; link the full report, Excel and detailed sources for inspection. The research workflow still covers all material analytical questions even when only selected findings are published.

## Minimalist house layout

Use the original, issuer-neutral [HTML template](../assets/one-page-brief.template.html) through [render_one_page.py](../scripts/render_one_page.py), starting with the [blank version-2 payload](../assets/one-page-brief.template.json). Presentation belongs in this shared template; company adapters supply selected prose and data.

- Template version 3.0.0: A4 portrait with 14 mm margins, white background, navy text, teal section emphasis and restrained rating/return colors. Written labels and signed values preserve meaning without color.
- Compact issuer/date header and recommendation badge; one investment takeaway in a larger headline; three prominent price/target/return figures. Use weights and spacing for hierarchy instead of numbered rows or boxes around every topic.
- Narrative sections run in one reading sequence. Use an optional compact financial table and a small same-horizon scenario strip. No decorative chart or fixed word/section quota is required. Unused space is preferable to filler.
- Segoe UI/Arial body 10.8 pt, section headings 10.2 pt, financial figures 9.5 pt, sources 8.5 pt. Minimum readable body/table fonts remain 10.5/9 pt. Mobile stacks the same content without horizontal overflow.
- Shorten low-value prose if the page is too long. Never hide, clip, truncate, reduce type/scale or export only page one. Material qualifications and counterevidence survive the edit.

## Payload and verification

1. Establish the current decision, frozen model outputs and evidence before drafting. A communication edit does not authorize changing forecasts, valuation or the call.
2. Draft only decision-useful content, applying the [argument sequence](natural-writing.md#argument-sequence): point/data, derivation, significance and stock-view implication. Develop that reasoning naturally under freely selected headings. Compare the final wording with the evidence and numerical record; keep uncertainty, signs, units, periods and source attribution.
3. Use payload `schemaVersion: 2.0.0`, a nonempty `headline`, and substantive `sections` with unique safe IDs, a title, paragraphs and source IDs. Supply `dividendYield` (forward annual yield as a decimal, or null when unknown). Optional `keyMetrics` (up to four short strings, in order: forward P/E, P/B, dividend yield, ROIC, e.g. "14.0x forward P/E") replace the default dividend-yield-only line under the prices; include the dividend yield when it is known. Optional `bearBasis`, `targetBasis` and `bullBasis` give each case's valuation, such as "15.0x NTM EPS of $2.40", printed under its price. The brief says "12-month target" rather than printing the target date. Section names/count are not prescribed. Omit `financials` when it adds no value; if used, supply its `sectionId`, caption, columns and rows. Sources and footnotes are optional and normally left out: link the full report, where sources are cited. Support links remain explicit.
4. Render with `python skills/research-brief/scripts/render_one_page.py --input company/brief.json --output company/brief.html`. Validation protects decision arithmetic, IDs, citations and table shape; it cannot judge the quality or completeness of the investment argument.
5. Use the production publication path in report-layout to print with Chrome, check payload-to-HTML consistency and actual PDF pagination, then perform the fresh visual review.
6. The release check verifies that the packaged brief is exactly one A4 page. Render the final PDF to an image and inspect it, and inspect the mobile view after visible changes. A page-count pass does not establish visual quality or analytical sufficiency.

Preserve user-authored edits during narrow revisions. Reconcile them with the payload before regeneration. If an open PDF is locked, save a clearly versioned output and update its links and current pointer rather than silently leaving a stale deliverable.
