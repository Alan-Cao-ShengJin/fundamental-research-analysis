# Financial red flags and reporting reliability

Use this procedure during the early financial-quality screen, deepen triggered issues in the model audit, and revisit them during thesis validation. A focused red-flag request can use filings directly without constructing a full forecast.

## Priority and evidence

Treat any evidence-supported threat to minority value — governance abuse or unreliable accounts — as high priority; either can decide the case. Assess severity, materiality and confidence rather than assigning a fixed risk hierarchy.

A flag is an observation requiring judgment, not a fraud verdict. Separate reporting error, aggressive but permitted estimates, business deterioration, cash restriction, governance abuse and established misconduct. Record severity independently of confidence: a potentially catastrophic issue may remain unverified. Prefer precise risk language; attribute any established finding to the regulator, court, exchange, auditor or other specific authoritative record and preserve its scope, date, appeal and remediation status. An investigation or short-seller allegation is not a finding. Do not presume issuer filings infallible when reporting reliability is the question.

| Priority | Meaning and response |
| --- | --- |
| P0: critical | Evidence-supported threat to minority property/value access, core reporting reliability, a material asset, solvency or going concern. Investigate before relying on the affected baseline or Buy case. An extreme ratio alone does not qualify. |
| P1: material | A supported anomaly could materially impair earnings, assets, cash returns or financing. Test plausible explanations and reflect a bounded loss/range or retain the essential unresolved issue. |
| P2: watch | A weaker, smaller or adequately explained signal warrants a defined follow-up. Do not inflate its severity to fill a risk section. |

Neither a numerical score nor several benign indicators can offset a decisive issue. No mandatory 100-point rating, fixed ratio cutoff or automatic exclusion for ordinary missing data applies. Missing required disclosures may itself be a concern; information not normally public is an access limitation. No flag observed means only within the stated review coverage.

## Screen the business and statements together

Start with original annual/interim statements, notes and audit reports, then relevant subsequent announcements, inquiry replies, restatements, control disclosures and auditor/CFO changes. Use enough history for the operating cycle, acquisitions and prior problems, while incorporating current developments. Do not require a fixed number of years when a defensible assessment is possible. Use independent operating, customer/supplier, legal or regulatory evidence when accessible and decision-relevant; a vendor repeating the filing is not corroboration of its truth.

Before treating a trend as suspicious, reconcile currency/scale, fiscal and cumulative periods, parent versus consolidated scope, acquisitions/disposals and restatement vintage. Bridge material policy, estimate, standard and presentation changes. Preserve original and comparable values separately; unresolved comparability stays explicit. Adapt tests to financial institutions, client assets, regulated cash, long-cycle contracts, seasonality and the applicable accounting framework. Do not impose industrial-company cash/debt or sales-cash thresholds on every sector.

| Area | Patterns worth testing | Discriminating follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash and debt | Large cash with persistent borrowing/refinancing, unexpectedly low income, opaque restrictions or poor payment capacity | Apply the cash procedure below; reconcile legal entity, currency, timing, restrictions and obligations. |
| Revenue and collection | Receivables/contract assets outgrow revenue, aging worsens as provisions fall, unusual period-end sales, returns or opaque counterparties | Test recognition, credit terms, subsequent collection, returns, tax/settlement basis, customer economics and possible circular funding. |
| Inventory and margins | Stock builds as demand weakens, reserve rates fall, margins improve without supported mix/cost changes | Compare physical volumes, utilization, sell-through, aging, realizable values and matched peer definitions. |
| Capex and other assets | Long-stalled construction, rising capitalized development, weak output despite heavy spending, acquisitions without cash returns | Trace additions/transfers, counterparties, project milestones, useful lives, impairment assumptions and subsequent operating evidence. |
| Profit and cash flow | Persistent profit/cash divergence or unusually strong operating cash unsupported by customer collection | Reproduce the bridge; inspect factoring/recourse, supplier finance, delayed payments, classification and period-end transfers. Operating cash flow can also be dressed up. |
| Accounting judgments | Profit-helping changes in provisions, useful lives, capitalization, fair values, deferred taxes or recurring 'one-offs' | Quantify the effect, check permissible treatment and commercial basis, and test less favorable defensible assumptions. |
| Group and related parties | Unexplained advances, guarantees, prepayments, intercompany balances or parent/subsidiary profit/cash gaps | Share the issue with governance; trace beneficiaries, enforceability, settlement and whether the same cash/transaction is counted twice. |
| Audit and controls | Scope limitations, adverse/disclaimer opinions, unexplained resignations/delays, repeated corrections or severe control failures | Read exact affected accounts, cause, amounts and subsequent remediation. Key audit matters, emphasis paragraphs and an unmodified opinion are not interchangeable conclusions. |

Require a coherent pattern and economic explanation, not a count of triggered ratios. Strong margins or foreign sales alone are not suspicious without contrary evidence. Clean audits reduce some concerns but do not prove fraud absent; going-concern uncertainty is a solvency warning, not proof of fabrication.

## Test reported cash before crediting excess cash

Distinguish four questions: does the balance exist, does the issuer own it, can the relevant legal entity use it, and can value reach common shareholders? A failure of access does not prove nonexistence; reported cash is not automatically distributable excess cash.

1. **Reconcile definitions and location.** Bridge balance-sheet cash/monetary funds to cash-flow cash equivalents, restricted/pledged deposits, deposits outside cash equivalents and investment products. Map material holders, currencies and jurisdictions where disclosed; separate client/escrow cash from issuer funds. Avoid double-counting deposits or investments across cash/net-debt schedules.
2. **Explain cash alongside debt.** Compare gross debt, maturities, coupons, covenants and financing needs with usable cash by entity/currency and relevant dates. Test seasonal working capital, low-cost term funding, currency needs, regulatory requirements, restricted subsidiary funds and credible committed investment. Persistent economically costly borrowing despite supposedly available cash deserves more scrutiny than the mere coexistence of both balances.
3. **Check yield on a matched basis.** Compare actual interest income with reasonably matched average interest-bearing balances, currency/rate environment, deposit terms and accrual/withholding treatment. Endpoint averages are rough if balances moved around period end; use better disclosed averages when available. Separate deposit interest from investment/fair-value income. A low apparent yield is a question, not proof that the cash is missing; a normal yield does not independently confirm existence.
4. **Trace movements and real uses.** Bridge beginning cash through operating, investing, financing, FX and perimeter changes to ending cash. Inspect unexplained round trips, temporary period-end inflows, related-party transfers, repeated equity/debt raises, subsequent payments/defaults and distributions. Check explanations for hoarding or continuing financing. A dividend or later payment demonstrates that amount's use at that time, not the authenticity of the entire reported balance.
5. **Assess assurance and contrary evidence.** Read audit qualifications/scope limitations, disclosed confirmation problems, exchange inquiries, restrictions and subsequent corrections. Use public external records when available. Do not claim bank confirmation, bank-statement access or audit-working-paper review without actually having it. Their ordinary absence from public filings is not itself evidence of fraud. Management reassurance and provider repetition do not resolve an identified contradiction.
6. **Carry the consequence into value.** Record reported cash, identifiable restrictions/obligations, estimated usable/excess cash and material unknowns separately. Use supported ranges or explicit adverse scenarios for questioned balances and associated debt/contingent liabilities. Do not credit uncertain cash at face value, presume all cash fictitious, subtract the same restriction twice, or manufacture a precise authenticity haircut. If a material baseline cannot be bounded meaningfully, the dependent valuation remains incomplete.

## Keep one issue record through the workflow

Record each material issue with evidence, plausible explanations, forecast/value effect and status. Link the same root cause across governance and financial quality to avoid duplicate charges; retain useful source locators.

Before a severe conclusion, reconstruct the pivotal source/calculation, check corrected filings and plausible benign explanations, and test whether alleged issues have actually been remediated. Record the review performed; self-review is not independent assurance and no extra agent is required. Do not erase a substantive risk simply by softening its wording.

Resolved issues need evidence, not management promises alone. Ordinary uncertainty can coexist with a complete, reasoned call; missing essentials preventing meaningful valuation belong in `criticalUnknowns`. A robust adverse finding can justify rejecting a Buy case without proving legal fraud. Do not invent a target or change it to force a label: apply the [decision contract](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md).

In the investor brief, show only decision-changing flags, their shareholder consequence and what would change the assessment. Keep the account-level evidence and test log linked as support; no mandatory scorecards, long audit report, boilerplate disclaimers or internal check counts. Preserve the full-company investment judgment.
