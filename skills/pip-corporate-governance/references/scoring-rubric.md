# Evidence-based governance assessment

Assess the current board, executives and controlling owners on a stated date. Keep the underlying events and mechanisms visible. A qualitative assessment with confidence and material exceptions is the default; use a numerical score when requested or when consistent, sufficiently complete evidence makes comparison useful.

## Dimensions

The optional weights preserve a consistent six-dimension comparison. Score each covered dimension from 0 to 100, then calculate `sum(weight * score)` with weights expressed as decimals.

| Dimension | Weight | Stronger evidence | Weaker evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership, rights and control resilience | 15% | Transparent beneficial control, clear economic/voting rights, workable succession and protections | Opaque control, discriminatory rights, contested control or pledges that threaten financing/control |
| Board oversight and controls | 20% | Demonstrated independent challenge, responsive audit oversight and effective remediation | Persistent material weaknesses, ineffective oversight or recurring unresolved reporting failures |
| Treatment of outside shareholders | 20% | Fair related-party terms, equitable issuance and defensible treatment across share classes | Documented value diversion, preferential transfers or coercive dilution/transactions |
| Disclosure and conduct | 20% | Consistent, timely reporting, candid corrections and resolution of past issues | Misleading disclosure, substantiated misconduct or material unresolved integrity concerns |
| Shareholder returns | 10% | Sustainable distributions or well-supported reinvestment that increases per-share value | Unproductive cash retention, poorly funded payouts or buybacks overwhelmed by unexplained dilution |
| Strategy and capital allocation | 15% | Disciplined investment, accountable acquisition outcomes and financing matched to returns | Repeated value-destructive expansion, unsupported rescue financing or controller-serving allocation |

An insider sale, ownership category, low dividend, litigation allegation or volatile stock price does not determine a score. Consider context, materiality, remediation and alternative explanations. Retaining cash to satisfy regulated capital or fund attractive reinvestment can serve shareholders.

## Coverage and comparison

- Attach source/date, observation, reasoning and confidence to each judgement. State the searched jurisdictions and time window for negative findings.
- Mark uncovered dimensions **unassessed**. Do not assign zero or silently redistribute their weights. With material coverage gaps, omit the aggregate/letter rating and report the partial assessment.
- Choose relevant comparators by business model, legal/shareholder regime, control structure and capital needs. Apply the same method and cutoff. A large, state-owned, foreign or popular company is not automatically a governance benchmark.
- If a coarse letter summary is useful, use A for 85-100, B for 70-<85, C for 55-<70 and D for <55. Explain that these are analytical categories, not statistical confidence levels, an integrity certification or a stock recommendation.
- Prefer a justified range when judgement is uncertain; do not label an arbitrary plus/minus interval as statistical confidence. Avoid decimals unsupported by the evidence.

## Decision relevance

Report the strongest/weakest dimensions, material exceptions and what they mean for outside owners. A severe rights, cash-access or conduct issue remains prominent even when other dimensions score well. No mechanical score threshold determines the stock call, the discount rate or the target price.

Translate the findings into cash flows, dilution, allocation, required return or the persistence of a valuation discount, with explicit assumptions and no duplicate charges for the same risk. Identify the actor/event that could improve outcomes and evidence of actual implementation. Where no credible improvement or shareholder-return path exists, an apparent bargain may remain unattractive.
