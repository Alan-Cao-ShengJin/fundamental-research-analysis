# Synthetic research review pack

All entities, observations and dates below are invented. Use only this pack as evidence, at a cutoff of 6 November 2026. The source observations are stipulated for this evaluation; the draft statements are claims to assess. The cases are independent.

## Task

Use the research-challenge skill and relevant owning references to review the draft statements. Give the relevant calculations, your supported interpretation, the strongest remaining alternative and how the investment implication changes. Preserve conclusions that the evidence supports. This is an opinion review: no full initiation, new stock rating, portfolio sizing, external search or PDF is required.

## Case A

Source A1, same date and FY2027 forecast window, USD millions, parent-attributable equity claims:

| Observation | Company A | Company B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market equity value | 100 | 90 |
| Usable surplus cash, with no associated debt | 20 | 30 |
| Attributable recurring net income, including cash income | 10 | 10 |
| After-tax income from that surplus cash | 1 | 1 |

There are no listed stakes, preferred claims or other necessary adjustments. Accounting and lease treatment match. Growth durability and operating risk have not been established as equal.

Draft: "Company A's operating business trades at 8.9x earnings, below Company B's 9.0x. This proves the market values A's operating business more cheaply. A 12x multiple produces a value close to our primary target, so the second method independently confirms it." The 12x input was selected by solving backward from the primary target; no separate evidence for it is supplied.

## Case B

Source B1, comparable full-year recognized revenue in USD millions; average active locations use the same definition in both years:

| Observation | Year 1 | Year 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue attributed to physical locations | 80 | 72 |
| Online revenue, separately attributable | 20 | 40 |
| Average active locations | 10 | 9 |

There were no acquisitions. Surviving-location transactions, price/mix, closure costs and margins are not disclosed.

Draft: "Revenue per location rose 24%, proving stronger physical-location demand and higher margins. We extend the same productivity growth to the remaining locations." The forecast uses total revenue divided by physical locations.

## Case C

Source C1: a controlled investment company bought an asset for 30; its current supported market value is 27. The controller approved the transaction using a legally permitted process that did not require independent shareholders' approval. The same process is available for future transactions. No other allocation history or demonstrated change in safeguards is supplied.

Draft: "The transaction cost shareholders 3. Therefore its entire governance effect is capped at 3; the remaining assets deserve an undiscounted peer multiple. We will reconsider the decision process only if a second poor acquisition occurs."

## Case D

Source D1: a cyclical company's current share price is 20 and comparable selected forward EPS is 2. In a downturn case EPS falls to 0.5 and a supported asset-based valuation gives a share price of 10. Current and scenario debt, cash and share claims are reconciled. The shares are not valued by applying a P/E in this case.

Draft: "The adverse case is invalid because its implied P/E is above today's. Cap the bear P/E at today's and lower the price accordingly."

## Case E

Source E1: a stable distributor has a share price of 80, 20 million shares, sustainable annual DPS of 4, annual operating cash of 160 million and maintenance capex of 40 million. There are no material additional cash obligations or growth capex requirements. A matched-market benchmark supports a 4% required yield; assume no material change in funding risk or payout rights. There is no usable P/E history. These are one-year forecast cash amounts, and the dividend is not yet ex-date.

Draft: "Annual dividends of 80 million have 1.5x free-cash-flow cover. At the supported 4% required yield, the distribution supports a price of 100, a 25% price gain if recognized. The prospective dividend yield is 5%, separate from that price target."

## Case F

Source F1: a bank has market equity value of 100, book common equity of 80 and recurring attributable net income of 10. Cash of 60 supports its operating liquidity against customer deposits of 500. None of that cash has been established as excess or distributable. Asset quality, capital requirements and the cost of equity need assessment.

Draft: "Strip the 60 of cash: the bank's core business is worth 40 and trades at 4x earnings, making it cheap against industrial businesses."

## Follow-up

For Case A, a new draft states: "We use a historical mean because forecast earnings grow at the same rate as in that window." The same source shows equal one-year growth, but the forecast now depends on two years of exceptional margins, whereas the historical window had diversified recurring cash returns; required financing costs have also risen. Explain whether growth alone settles the anchor, what can still be concluded, and the work needed before retaining or changing it. Do not automatically declare either permanent impairment or mean reversion.
