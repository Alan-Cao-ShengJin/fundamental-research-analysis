# Sector mechanism selection and model checks

These are authored forward-evaluation cases, not claims of completed agent runs.
Arithmetic miniatures are tested separately in `tests/test_sector_plans.py`.

**Sector name versus economic exposure.** Prompt: "Research this mining-technology group." Supply a machinery manufacturer, a small owned solar/PPA portfolio, EPC contracts and a financed equipment-sales receivable. Expect industrial equipment/project/aftermarket mechanisms plus only the material generation/credit detail. Do not assign the entire company a mine-grade/reserve NAV, treat battery factories as regulated utilities, or count EPC and owned-project revenue/capex on the same project twice. Select by actual activities and explain gaps.

**Bank capital and credit.** Prompt: "Build a bank forecast and assess its dividend." Supply average assets/funding, gross charge-offs and recoveries, loan-stage migration, RWA, capital and local distribution requirements. Expect a matched-period NII, allowance and capital roll-forward, a feasible dividend and equity-based valuation. Do not add recoveries twice after net charge-offs, subtract operating deposits as industrial net debt, or infer distribution capacity from accounting cash alone.

**Fabrication output versus capacity.** Prompt: "Update this chip producer's capacity model." Supply both nameplate capacity and actual wafer starts, utilisation, good dies/wafer and separate wafer/packaged-chip sales. Expect a choice of a compatible production chain, inventories and linked capital/absorption costs. Do not multiply actual starts by utilisation again, apply die yield to contract wafer revenue without a pricing basis, or treat a design win as recognised sales.

**Clinical rights and correlated risks.** Prompt: "Value a clinical-stage company." Supply a licensed geographic right, two same-mechanism programmes, conditional trial probabilities, current committed costs, contingent later costs, launch/expiry dates and runway. Expect a patient/net-price/rights funnel and a timed probability tree, funding/dilution and competition. Do not calculate rNPV as peak sales times probability, apply cumulative success twice, assume independent asset outcomes, or probability-discount current committed spending as if it were conditional.

**Payment volume and credit seasoning.** Prompt: "Explain why payment growth is not reaching profit." Supply gross/net take definitions, rebates, merchant mix, customer-money liabilities and rapid loan originations with cohort loss curves. Expect net fee/incentive and aged-vintage analysis with funding effects. Do not call gross TPV revenue, count incentives twice, treat newly originated loans as evidence that mature losses improved, or add safeguarded customer balances to excess equity cash.

**Spectrum, leases and dividend coverage.** Prompt: "Can this telecom fund its dividend after the network upgrade?" Supply monthly ARPU, average subscriber counts, EBITDA already after cash leases, network capex, spectrum instalments, tax/interest and dividends. Expect compatible time units, lease deductions once and cash-available/dividends coverage. A ratio below one is a shortfall; omit neither spectrum nor committed network spending. Do not extrapolate a deferred-capex trough as perpetual cash flow.

**Airline units and capital obligations.** Prompt: "Forecast revenue and cash at a different load factor." Supply ASK, RPK yield, passenger RASK, fuel-inclusive and fuel-exclusive CASK alternatives, aircraft commitments and leases already recognised under the accounting basis. Expect revenue through either ASK times passenger RASK or ASK times load times RPK yield, with compatible costs and funding. Do not apply load twice, deduct fuel twice, recapitalise recognised leases twice, or ignore aircraft delivery commitments.

**Examples do not certify research.** Prompt: "Use the sector example to initiate coverage." Supply only an independently invented miniature and no issuer history or current sources. Expect useful model/source planning with an explicit synthetic boundary, not an issuer forecast, estimated target or completed research claim. A missing foundational input stays missing; the example's clean arithmetic does not supply regulatory, clinical, demand or valuation evidence.
