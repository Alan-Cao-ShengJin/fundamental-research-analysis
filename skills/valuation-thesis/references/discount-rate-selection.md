# Select the discount rate from current evidence

Use whenever a valuation or capital-return comparison needs a cost of equity or WACC. The analyst system retrieves the inputs, chooses the method and derives the rate autonomously. Do not ask the user to pick the premium, beta or discount rate. A user-specified required return is a mandate to respect and distinguish from a market-calibrated estimate.

## Retrieve, calibrate and calculate

1. **Match the cash-flow claim.** Determine valuation currency, nominal versus real flows, equity versus enterprise claim, forecast duration and research cutoff. Select the rate for those cash flows, not simply the listing venue. Use the [approved source routes](../../evidence-intake/references/research-data-providers.md#approved-sources) and retain original dated observations. Fixed evidence packs retain their declared boundary.
2. **Retrieve the risk-free anchor.** Use a dated currency-consistent benchmark with an appropriate tenor; a ten-year government yield is a starting convention, not a value remembered from another study. Check sovereign default and inflation content where material. Do not treat a risky sovereign yield as default-free and charge its default premium again elsewhere. Record observation and publication dates; later data cannot enter a historical cutoff.
3. **Calibrate the equity premium and business exposure.** Prefer a transparent, dated market-implied estimate where available and appropriate. Assess its method, geography and underlying risk-free rate; historical estimates or close-market proxies are alternatives with limitations. Assess operating-country exposure, financing and business risk rather than automatically using the listing country's premium. If beta is useful, select a reliable matched estimate or economically comparable peer estimate, accounting for leverage and benchmark differences. A noisy recent stock regression is not automatically superior to a supported peer estimate. Do not add country risk already included in the chosen premium.
4. **Derive one supported central rate and material alternatives.** Explain the method before comparing its valuation result. For a beta-based build, show `cost of equity = risk-free rate + beta × market equity premium + non-overlapping additional adjustments`. A transparent additive build is acceptable when beta evidence is weak; explain its calibration. Avoid arbitrary rounding to a familiar rate, automatic conservatism and fitting the rate to the share price or desired call. Do not charge risks twice through both cash-flow scenarios and rate adjustments. For enterprise cash flows, build WACC from the equity component, dated cost of debt, tax treatment and justified financing weights; do not substitute cost of equity for WACC.

## Keep the model live and reviewable

Store sourced inputs in the evidence/data area, with dates, units, method and source identifiers. Link the selected inputs into a visible rate build beside valuation, with the resulting rate feeding the calculation. Keep independently editable assumptions in one location. On a new research cutoff, refresh relevant market inputs and reassess company risk; preserve the earlier build to explain the change. During a frozen run use the pinned evidence vintage rather than silently retrieving a later observation. Market-rate changes can revise valuation without changing operating forecasts.

The numerical reverse-DCF helper consumes the system's resolved inputs. Its requirement for explicit numbers is an interface requirement, never a request for human selection. With a beta-based build, its additive `riskAdjustmentPct` can contain `(beta - 1) × market premium` plus justified additional adjustments; show that decomposition in the model. Supply `riskFreePct`, `equityRiskPremiumPct` and the calculated adjustment, or a directly linked total. Its ±1-point note prompts explanation, not a ceiling or instruction to ask the user. The helper validates arithmetic; retrieval and economic selection belong to the analyst workflow.

If the preferred feed is unavailable, autonomously choose a defensible dated alternative or clearly labeled estimate and test a meaningful range. Record an essential unresolved input if no defensible estimate exists; leave an optional DCF cross-check unavailable rather than interrupting otherwise supportable research to demand a user-chosen rate. The model reader checks source vintage, currency/claim matching, calibration, double counting and material sensitivity.

Method references: [Damodaran's risk manual](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/papers/RiskManual.pdf) for matching cash flows and required returns; [country-risk methodology](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/ctryprem.html) for distinguishing market and country premiums. These explain methods; their examples and current figures are not defaults or point-in-time evidence for another cutoff.
