# Select the valuation method and test repricing

Choose the primary tool from the business model, shareholder claim, lifecycle, cash visibility, cyclicality and capital requirements. Follow the [valuation yardstick](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md#valuation-yardstick) by company type and explain the choice for the issuer. P/E is the most widely used. A DCF is at most a cross-check, only for companies with predictable cash flow.

Follow the [target and earnings-basis contract](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md#target-and-earnings-basis) and [calendar guide](valuation-calendar.md).

Read [valuation drivers and dividends](valuation-driver-and-dividends.md) to decide whether earnings growth, a higher multiple, cash distributions or asset realisation drives the target. For dividend-led cases, quantify payout, yield and funded coverage before selecting a required yield. P/E and dividend yield on the same payout are algebraically linked, not independent valuation support.

## Economic fit

For a normalized-return or asset method, record the target asset/book period separately from the period/transition supplying the sustainable return. Explain why that normalization is representative and quantify a material plausible alternative. A later sustainable return is not automatically a period error, and not permission to shift a P/E target's current-forward EPS. Retain those distinctions in the [decision checkpoint](../../report-layout/references/decision-record.md#material-horizon-choices); do not select a horizon to cross a rating threshold.

Apply [advantage-duration and capital-return analysis](../../company-model/references/competition-and-capital-returns.md) before selecting mature margins or a target multiple. Explain competitive response and how growth is funded. Reconcile terminal growth with marginal returns/reinvestment under stated steady-state assumptions, and test a shorter advantage period. Report material terminal-value dependence for DCF; for multiples, explain the durability and returns the chosen rating rewards. A method cross-check should test economics rather than reproduce the same unsupported premise.

| Business economics | Suitable starting methods and checks |
| --- | --- |
| Established nonfinancial company | Forward P/E or enterprise multiples against comparable peers and own history; a DCF only as a cross-check when cash flow is predictable. Reconcile debt, leases and other claims. |
| Banks and regulated finance | Residual income, capital-distribution/dividend capacity, normalised P/E or P/B linked to sustainable ROE and cost of equity. Deposits are funding, and required capital is retained investment; an industrial EV bridge may mislead. |
| Insurance | Earnings, book/residual income or disclosed embedded/value-of-business measures appropriate to the products and accounting. Reconcile capital and shareholder claims. |
| Property, investment holdings and portfolios | NAV, asset cash flows or sum of parts, reflecting taxes, debt, holding costs and realisability. Distribution metrics need comparable definitions and maintenance investment. |
| Cyclicals and commodities | Mid-cycle earnings/cash flows, asset/NAV or replacement economics where useful. Do not capitalise peak margins or compare meaningless loss-driven multiples. |
| Finite contracts or depleting assets | Cash flows over supported remaining lives; renewal/extension is a separate scenario, not an automatic perpetuity. |
| Early-stage or temporarily unprofitable business | Scenario cash flows and unit economics; revenue/gross-profit multiples only with a credible margin, financing and dilution bridge. Growth alone does not establish equity value. |
| Diversified group | Sum of parts using appropriate methods for different businesses. Reconcile shared costs, intercompany balances, parent debt, taxes and ownership; avoid double counting cash and earnings. |

These are starting points, not a rigid sector lookup. An operating model and cash/financing audit remain necessary even when a multiple is the primary method. Missing essential inputs limit the valuation claim.

Use the relevant [sector activity reference](../../company-model/references/sectors/index.md) for the mechanism-specific units, cash/capital boundaries and traps behind the method. A miner's finite assets, a clinical probability tree, a bank's retained regulatory capital and an industrial backlog need different cash/claim treatments. These references do not impose extra methods or replace the selected valuation and existing decision rules.

## Peer valuation

For full company research, examine relevant peers as a distinct default angle. Select by revenue model, growth, margins, returns on capital/equity, capital intensity, geography and shareholder rights, rather than industry labels alone. State coverage and exclusions; weak comparability is a limitation.

Align valuation dates, currencies, fiscal/forward periods, dilution, accounting, leases and adjusted/reported earnings. Retain sources and estimate vintages. Explain an issuer premium or discount through sustainable economics and governance. Neither the highest peer multiple nor the median is automatically fair value.

Match the asset and income boundary as well as the ratio's name. A residual operating-business P/E after removing cash and listed stakes is not directly comparable with a peer's whole-equity P/E. Show reported and comparable ratios with the asset/liability adjustments and the matching attributable income removed or retained; use the same treatment for each company. Likewise match consolidated versus attributable profit, regional versus group margins, and lease treatment. If a material adjustment cannot be supported, keep the comparison directional rather than using it to quantify a discount. Do not strip operating cash/debt or required regulatory capital from banks and insurers as if they were industrial surplus assets.

A peer earnings table needs a common price date, explicit fiscal-period end, estimate basis and available coverage/vintage information. A shared label such as FY1 or FY2028 does not make different fiscal years the same window. Calendarize when support permits; otherwise identify the comparison as directional and do not infer a precision premium from it. Business-model and capital-intensity differences still require economic interpretation.

## The company's own historical forward valuation

Compare current forward valuation with the issuer's relevant historical forward multiples. Keep next-twelve-month, FY1 or FY2 horizons consistent and explain fiscal rolls. Historical observations require the forecasts available then; old prices divided by today's revised forecasts are not a historical forward series.

Build the band from monthly month-end prices over the consensus forward EPS current at each month-end. Pull the longest history available (ten years or since listing) first, then choose the cut-off: use the period since the business settled into its current shape, and say why. Start the window at the dated event the rationale names (a change in mix, growth regime, financing or control, as investors could first see it), not at a round lookback that merely approximates it. Show the meaningful alternatives beside it: the other defensible event starts and the conventional lookbacks, each with its mean, observations, target and rating. Choose on the economics, not on which window is shortest, gives the lowest or highest multiple or a particular rating. A window made of the recent de-rating is neither favoured nor excluded by its length: it fits when matched evidence (growth, returns, mix, financing) shows a new lasting regime, and not when the move reflects sentiment that the evidence does not support. When a defensible alternative crosses a rating threshold, say so where the target is argued and why the chosen window is the better guide. Drop years whose valuation was structurally different, such as a sector bubble, a different business mix or a pre-profit phase. Show the mean, ±1 and ±2 standard deviations, and today's P/E on consensus and on our EPS with its position in standard deviations. Consensus EPS for Chinese issuers mixes reported, recurring (excluding non-recurring items) and company-adjusted bases; apply the target multiple to our EPS on the basis the band was built on, and state which.

Reconcile splits, share counts, enterprise-value claims, accounting and perimeter changes. Choose a window relevant to the business and financing regime; distinguish changes in mix, growth, profitability, rates, leverage and governance. Exclude or explain near-zero/negative-earnings multiples and exceptional events. A past peak is not an entitlement to rerate.

Similar forecast growth to the historical window is one comparison, not proof that the old multiple remains appropriate. Test whether growth duration, reinvestment and returns, cash conversion, financing/rates, business mix and shareholder rights still support that regime. Explain the material differences and the strongest case for today's premium or discount persisting. A supported temporary dislocation retains the house mean-reversion default without requiring a new named catalyst; neither a statistical distance from the mean nor an unresolved regime question establishes that premise.

If estimate vintages are unavailable or listing history is short, state the limitation. Do not relabel trailing multiples, pre-IPO funding valuations or a current-forecast backfill as listed historical forward valuation. Use the supported peer/operating evidence instead.

A short or single-regime history (under about three years, or one controller, product cycle or market mood) cannot carry the target multiple alone. Cross-check it against the closest peers listed in the same market and doing the same business (same products, customers and listing venue before overseas or adjacent names): their forward P/E, growth and returns, where they sit in their own bands, and why this stock deserves a premium or discount to them. Show the longer history too and explain what changed. Say which anchor sets the target and how the others confirm or qualify it.

A trailing band is a separate cross-check: month-end price over the latest twelve-month EPS already reported at that date, switched on the announcement date rather than the period end. It is most useful where consensus history is short or analysts have a clear bias, and least useful for cyclicals or near-zero earnings. Compare it only with today's trailing P/E and never merge it with the forward band; applying a trailing multiple to forward EPS mixes earnings bases and needs explicit economic justification; it does not by itself determine the target date. Price over the earnings later reported for a forward window is hindsight: use it only to see whether past consensus was persistently optimistic or pessimistic, which can explain why a cautious forecast on the forward-band mean gives a low target. It is not a reason to raise the multiple by itself.

A provider-reported historical mean is a dated benchmark, not a reconstructed time series. Retain its publisher, report date, observation window, forward convention and access scope. For a calculated historical mean, retain the dated price/estimate observations, sampling frequency, valid/excluded counts and treatment of splits, missing or nonpositive earnings. Never manufacture a point-in-time forward history from trailing ratios or later-revised forecasts. A few valid annual observations are an annual sample, not a reconstructed monthly or daily series or a distribution. An indexed excerpt is not a full PDF read.

Apply the canonical [target-multiple rule](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md#target-and-earnings-basis) after testing whether growth, mix, cash conversion, capital returns, rates and governance support a temporary dislocation or a different regime. Statistical distance alone does not establish the premise. The decision contract owns the mean-reversion default and conditions for going above it; do not replace those conditions with an additional universal catalyst test. A bull-case range should reflect the normal supported regime, with its empirical size and economic rationale stated.

### Mixed or partial valuation changes

Partial recognition is already allowed by the decision contract; it needs calibration, not a new rating convention. Where part of a discount is temporary and part reflects changed economics, select a relevant regime/subsample or a normalized comparable anchor, explain the adjustment for durability, returns, cash, funding and rights, and test nearby alternatives. A fraction of historical standard deviation describes the selected distance; it does not independently justify its size. Do not fit a P/E-to-growth relationship merely to reproduce a preferred target: matched point-in-time observations, regime differences and predictive uncertainty matter, and correlation is not a pricing law.

If reasonable single-driver changes cross a rating threshold, state that sensitivity and why the central judgment remains preferable; reflect the margin for error in the separate new-money assessment. An adverse case changing the rating is normal and does not mechanically invalidate the central call. Preserve one supported primary target and its current-forward earnings basis.

### Align the forward convention before calculating a target

Match NTM, FY1 or FY2 and the earnings definition to the chosen benchmark at the relevant valuation date. If a dated annual forecast must be bridged to NTM, derive weights from the issuer's fiscal calendar and chosen earnings-window anchor date. Use supported quarterly detail where seasonality matters; an annual-weight approximation must be identified and tested where material. Do not hardcode one issuer's weights as a generic convention, silently roll another full fiscal year, or call the blended analyst forecast consensus.

State the earnings window used at each endpoint of the return bridge. Separate period progression from revisions and multiple change; follow the [calendar guide](valuation-calendar.md).

## Rerating and derating

For both higher and lower target multiples, explain where the selected number comes from: a dated comparable anchor plus justified adjustments, relevant point-in-time own-history evidence, or an explicit economic derivation. Show why that supports the selected level rather than merely saying that growth, brand or risk deserves a premium/discount. "Analyst judgment" labels an assumption; it does not calibrate its size. Current valuation is a market observation, not automatic fair value.

A forecast earnings shortfall does not itself prove that the multiple should contract. Explain how the evidence changes expected durability, growth, returns, funding risk or credibility, whether it is already expected, and what investors will observe. Where a valuation change lacks support and the stock is not temporarily away from its own mean, today’s multiple on appropriately dated forecast earnings is a legitimate central case. A peer median is an anchor to assess economically, not automatic fair value.

Rerating means investors paying a higher valuation, such as a higher P/E, for the company's earnings. Explain the reason before counting the gain. A peer's higher P/E does not establish that this will happen. A return to the stock's own mean after a temporary de-rating needs no further catalyst; a multiple above the mean needs a significant one.

For a target that relies materially on a higher P/E, answer:

1. **Why is it discounted today?** Identify the concern: weaker earnings, unreliable cash conversion, governance, a declining business, an unfavourable market regime or another supported reason.
2. **What changes that concern or its importance?** Specify the operating result, cash repayment, capital-allocation action, credible theme association or other development. Ordinary earnings delivery can matter when investors doubt it; explain the evidence for that doubt rather than assuming it.
3. **What will investors see, and when?** Identify the observable evidence and confirmed or estimated window. Results dates, "better recognition" and "confidence returns" are not causes by themselves. A hope of reform is not implemented reform.
4. **Why this target valuation?** Connect the change to earnings durability, growth, returns, shareholder distributions or lower risk. Use comparable peers and dated own-history evidence while retaining justified discounts. Explain what could stop or delay the reassessment.

State this causal argument in the pitch. If support is weak, keep rerating as an upside scenario rather than the central target. A bull-case multiple still needs these answers: analyse how the bull case could become reality rather than picking a number. Recalculate the base target and call; a footnote saying rerating is uncertain does not justify making it the whole Buy case. Do not change the target merely to meet the attractiveness hurdle.

Also show what investors earn **if they keep paying today's P/E**: the following fiscal year's EPS at today's multiple, labelled as an earnings-progression illustration with its dates and basis, with the dividend shown separately, and a case where they pay less. Use that wording in investor prose instead of unexplained unchanged-multiple jargon. Today's P/E on the same current-forward EPS is the reference price by construction and is not a finding.

On an explicitly selected comparable basis, `target price = selected forward EPS * justified target multiple`. State the earnings period and follow the [calendar guide](valuation-calendar.md). Separate:

- earnings effect: `(target EPS - starting EPS) * starting multiple`;
- multiple effect: `target EPS * (target multiple - starting multiple)`.

These sum to the modelled price change on a consistent share/earnings basis. Use the appropriate NAV/book/parts bridge for other methods, and show distributions separately. Use peer and historical forward evidence to justify the multiple range, and the [industry analysis](../../industry-analysis/SKILL.md) barrier and niche findings for how long excess returns last; persistent governance discounts may remain even after operating improvement. Record free float and average daily traded value: thin trading limits position size and can sustain a discount.

When starting and target earnings use the same forecast, the earnings contribution is zero and the price change comes from the multiple. Business delivery may support recognition, but it is not a second numerical contribution to add to the bridge.

Apply the four [target-underwriting checks](target-underwriting.md) before selecting the primary target.

## Select the primary target

Reconcile primary-method, peer-implied and own-history/rerating values on the same date/claim basis. Investigate disagreements in cash conversion, capital retention, normalisation, finite lives, rates and regime. Select the most suitable anchor and explain its weight; do not automatically average methods or choose whichever supports a preferred call.

Identify what a cross-check tests independently and what it shares with the primary valuation. Establish a parts multiple or other secondary anchor from its own evidence before comparing the answers; a target-implied input is a diagnostic, not independent confirmation. Agreement between methods using the same forecast, comparable multiple or risk assumption does not provide several independent proofs. Where the methods share an unsupported assumption, test that assumption rather than describing the similar prices as validation.

For a decision-driving multiple, required yield, discount rate, asset discount or recognition fraction, retain its actual derivation in the existing rationale: sourced observation, discretionary house assumption or reverse-solved implication of price. Explain independent economic support or present an honestly conditional judgment with material alternatives. A plausible bracket alone does not select a unique point inside it. Compare the historical derivation when the rationale changes; do not call a previously price-calibrated target an independent coincidence without new support. This does not require matching consensus, a specific cash haircut or a preferred call.

The full research retains method selection, peer comparison, historical forward evidence/gaps and the rerating bridge. The decision brief shows the selected target, pivotal valuation reason and material risks. All calculations consume current model outputs and dated comparable observations.
