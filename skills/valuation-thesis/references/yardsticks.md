# What kind of company, and how the buy side values it

Step one of the [valuation sequence](../SKILL.md#value-the-company-in-six-steps). Name the kind of company and the yardstick buy-side investors usually value it on. The rows are market conventions to start from, and they differ by market where noted. Confirm the yardstick in use for this stock from how investors and brokers discuss it at the research date.

## Using the list

- **Sector convention first.** A bank is valued on P/B whether or not it is growing. The profile table is for everything without its own convention.
- **The kind can change.** A grower matures into a yield stock; a cyclical is read on P/B at the trough and on earnings mid-cycle. The question is how investors treat the company now.
- **Mixed businesses** go to a sum of the parts, each part on its own row, at attributable ownership and with nothing counted twice.
- **No row fits.** Ask what the buyer is paying for: earnings (P/E, EV/EBITDA), cash returned (dividend or FCF yield), assets (P/B, NAV), a future not yet in earnings (EV/Sales, or out-year earnings discounted back) or an event (deal value).

The [sector cards](../../company-model/references/sectors/index.md) hold the operating mechanics for each kind of company, and each card names the yardstick for its kind, including kinds the sector table below does not list. A business with no card is modelled by the steps under [when no card fits](../../company-model/references/sectors/index.md#when-no-card-fits).

## By economic profile

| Kind of company | Main yardstick | Judged against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steady grower, visible earnings | Forward P/E | Own forward P/E band and close peers; FCF yield as support |
| Fast grower, profitable | Forward P/E read with growth (PEG), often on next year's or the year after's EPS | Growth rate and how long it lasts; peers with similar growth |
| Fast grower, loss-making or barely profitable | EV/Sales or EV/gross profit | Growth and margin at maturity, with the funding and dilution to get there; P/E on a profitable out-year discounted back |
| Tech company moving from loss to profit | EV/adjusted EBITDA, the measure the companies themselves put forward | What the adjustments leave out, share-based pay above all; P/E and FCF once reported profit is real |
| Mature, low growth, high payout | Dividend yield | Yields of comparable income stocks and bonds; payout cover ([dividend valuation](valuation-driver-and-dividends.md)) |
| Mature cash generator buying back shares | FCF yield, P/E | Dividend plus buyback yield |
| Cyclical | P/B against through-cycle ROE, or mid-cycle EPS × mid-cycle P/E | Own P/B band; replacement cost. P/E looks cheap at the peak and dear at the trough |
| Cyclical the market now treats as a structural grower | Forward P/E, sometimes on an out-year | How many years the story is expected to run; the old P/B yardstick if the cycle returns |
| Turnaround or recovery | P/E on recovered earnings two to three years out | EV/Sales × a normal margin; asset value as the floor |
| Heavily indebted or distressed | EV/EBITDA, with equity as what is left after debt | Asset cover, refinancing, liquidation value |
| Asset-rich, earnings understate value | Discount to NAV, P/B | Own historical discount; who unlocks the value and when |
| Holding company or conglomerate | Sum of the parts less a holding discount | Own historical discount to NAV; parent debt, tax and shared costs |
| Net-cash deep value | Ex-cash P/E, EV/EBIT, P/B | Whether the cash gets paid out |
| Theme stock, earnings far out | Out-year earnings × multiple, discounted back; or EV/Sales | Peers in the same theme; market appetite for it |
| Order-book or project business | P/E on the year the backlog delivers | Backlog cover, order intake |
| Finite-life asset or concession | DCF or NAV over the remaining life; dividend yield | Years left; renewal is a separate scenario |
| Deal target or special situation | Offer price × chance of completion | Spread to the offer; standalone value if it breaks |
| New listing, short history | Peer multiples | IPO comparables |

## How each yardstick is measured

The band in step two and the target in step four use the same measure. The forward period, and the rule that it is never rolled forward to the target date, are the same for every yardstick ([valuation calendar](valuation-calendar.md)).

| Yardstick | Band built from | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P/E | Price ÷ consensus forward EPS at each month-end | Multiple × our forward EPS |
| P/B | Price ÷ forward book value per share where the consensus history has it, otherwise the latest reported (say which); read beside ROE | Multiple × our book value per share on the same basis |
| EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, EV/adjusted EBITDA | Enterprise value at each date (market value plus net debt and other claims as then reported) ÷ the consensus forward figure | Multiple × our forward figure, less net debt and other claims, per share |
| Dividend yield | Forward DPS ÷ price | Our DPS ÷ the yield ([dividend valuation](valuation-driver-and-dividends.md#price-yield-and-the-investment-argument)) |
| Discount to NAV | Price ÷ NAV per share at each date | Our NAV per share × (1 − discount) |
| Sum of the parts | Each part on its own row | Parts added, less net debt and any holding discount, per share |

## When the yardstick switches

The yardstick for a company can change when the market's story about it changes. These are known patterns, not a closed list.

| From | To | What triggers it | Seen in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P/B | Forward P/E | The market decides a cycle has become multi-year growth | Memory on AI demand; solar and batteries in a boom; shipbuilders with a long order book |
| Forward P/E | P/B | The growth story breaks and the cycle comes back | Solar and batteries after the boom |
| P/B | Dividend yield | Capex stops and the cash is paid out | Coal producers |
| EV/Sales | EV/adjusted EBITDA, then P/E or FCF yield | A loss-making tech company matures | Ride-hailing, delivery and e-commerce platforms |
| P/E | EV/Sales | Cheap money, with growth valued over profit | Software when rates were near zero |
| Dividend yield | Growth P/E | A stable business gains a growth driver | Power generators on data-centre demand |
| P/E or NAV | P/B and balance-sheet survival | Growth ends and solvency becomes the question | Property developers in a credit squeeze |
| Pipeline NPV | P/E | A biotech's drugs reach the market | |

For every stock, answer three questions:

1. What kind of company is this, and what is its usual yardstick?
2. Is the market using that yardstick now, or has it switched?
3. If it has switched, what belief supports the switch, and what does the price become if the belief fails and the old yardstick returns? That answer is the natural bear case.
