# Target underwriting

Check the target before selecting it, with depth proportional to the decision:

1. Compare our target and EPS with consensus: how far below or above we are and why. Is the market missing something, or are we? Consensus is usually to be respected. Depart from it only when we know something the market does not and are confident, with conviction, that it will happen.
2. Explain why the stock trades at its current multiple, which variable and period set it ([what the market trades](../../research-cycle/references/what-the-market-trades.md)) and whether the reason is structural, temporary or unresolved, following the target-multiple guidance in the [decision contract](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md#target-and-earnings-basis). Test the relevant economics and risks, not growth similarity alone; resolve the strongest reason the old regime may no longer apply before relying on it.
3. Explain the realisable path within twelve months: earnings growth above expectations, consistent growth at a maintained multiple, or a yield re-rating. Without support for a multiple change, today's multiple on the appropriate forecast earnings is a legitimate central case.
4. Cross-check with the secondary methods that fit the company, normalizing asset/income boundaries and identifying shared assumptions under [method selection](method-selection.md). A secondary input backed out of the primary target does not independently support it. Choose a defensible target and apply the decision contract.

P/E is the more widely used yardstick. Historical forward P/E bands are useful: a stock that is temporarily oversold or overbought should return to its historical mean forward P/E eventually. Pushing above the mean requires a significant catalyst. Use a DCF only for companies with specific, predictable cash flow, as a cross-check.

Test a coherent adverse resolution of the actual debate, including its interaction with valuation, funding, dilution and access to cash where material. A historical-band offset is not a substitute for that resolution, and a bear price is not a loss floor. If an adverse case assumes a higher comparable multiple than today's, explain the economic or denominator reason rather than silently granting a recovery. Do not impose a universal multiple cap: trough earnings can raise P/E while value falls. Show a more severe plausible outcome separately if the three central scenarios omit a material tail, and qualify probabilities to the outcomes they actually cover.
