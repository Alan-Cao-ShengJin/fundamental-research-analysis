# Target underwriting

Check the target before selecting it, with depth proportional to the decision:

1. Compare our target and EPS with consensus: how far below or above we are and why. Is the market missing something, or are we? Consensus is usually to be respected. Depart from it only when we know something the market does not and are confident, with conviction, that it will happen.
2. Explain why the stock trades at its current multiple and whether the reason is structural or temporary, following the target-multiple guidance in the [decision contract](../../investment-conclusion/references/decision-contract.md#target-and-earnings-basis).
3. Explain the realisable path within twelve months: earnings growth above expectations, consistent growth at a maintained multiple, or a yield re-rating. Without support for a multiple change, today's multiple on the appropriate forecast earnings is a legitimate central case.
4. Cross-check with the secondary methods that fit the company. Choose a defensible target and apply the decision contract.

P/E is the more widely used yardstick. Historical forward P/E bands are useful: a stock that is temporarily oversold or overbought should return to its historical mean forward P/E eventually. Pushing above the mean requires a significant catalyst. Use a DCF only for companies with specific, predictable cash flow, as a cross-check.
