# Current company and industry expectations

## Company comparison

1. Freeze the cutoff, exact security/share class, price, share count/dilution, currency and equity/enterprise bridge. Date consensus estimates and attribute each analyst view; inaccessible research remains a stated coverage gap.
2. Retrieve audited operating/cash-flow outputs and the governance assessment. Explain who pays, the core business engine and moat, then the narrative's path: share/volume → realised price/mix → margins → reinvestment → cash accessible to outside shareholders. Define issuer-specific terms and conditions that could refute the case.
3. Select the primary method by economic fit using [method selection](method-selection.md); examine relevant peers and the issuer's own historical forward valuation. Translate price into conditional expectations using the suitable earnings, asset, capital or cash-flow bridge. Use the [cash-flow method](valuation-method.md) when applicable, not universally. Keep assumptions explicit and report reverse-solution non-uniqueness.
4. Compare price-implied conditions, attributed published views and independent scenarios. In expectations-only work, an analyst's own view is optional and labelled. For full recommendation work, identify the pivotal supported expectations gap and its price/cash-return mechanism. Test whether governance explains a persistent discount; do not invent consensus or assume that cheapness alone creates a catalyst.
5. Return view/source/date, driver path, cash-flow basis, governance consequences, assumptions, supporting/counterevidence and next test, pinned to model/market versions. For the full company route, pass a selected primary target/date and same-horizon bear/bull targets to conclusion; a valuation range does not replace the eventual decision block.

For published target prices, use the same conditional model to make assumptions comparable when feasible, but also preserve the original analyst's stated valuation method and horizon. Translating a P/E or SOTP target into a DCF scenario does not establish that the analyst used that scenario. For negative/near-zero baselines, omit misleading growth multiples and provide meaningful amounts or ranges instead.

## Industry/value-chain mode

For full company work and material industry comparisons, include [market context and narratives](../../pip-macro-event-analysis/references/market-regime-and-investor-base.md) alongside operating value, governance and price/payoff. Scale its importance to the decision rather than recent conversation topics. Distinguish topical association from economic exposure; the former can affect a multiple before earnings emerge. Keep dated attention/positioning/flow and investor-access checks proportionate. Unknown buyers do not invalidate a reasoned narrative-price scenario, and popularity does not establish attractive payoff.

Start from a user-provided company list or a sourced [theme shortlist](../../pip-theme-screening/SKILL.md). State coverage, exclusions and whether the sample is representative. Batch work to fit evidence and calculation capacity while preserving consistent definitions across companies.

Compare as-of date, fiscal periods, FX, accounting perimeter, cash-flow definition and valuation basis. Avoid comparing enterprise multiples directly with equity multiples. Show upstream/downstream revenue dependence, customer/supplier bargaining power and margin transfer; do not sum transactions along a value chain as independent end demand. Cross-market comparisons also identify share-class rights, accessibility and material market-structure differences.

Highlight where expected growth/profit pools concentrate and which assumptions compete for the same economics. Separate business exposure from market expectations. Choose the primary valuation by business economics; peer and own historical forward valuations support justified multiples/discounts and the rerating case rather than being automatically subordinate to DCF.

## Optional keyword attention analysis

Use only an accessible, documented corpus. Fix coverage, document types, deduplication, language and synonym clusters before comparison. For each month:

- denominator = unique company-related documents in the covered corpus;
- numerator = those documents containing the specified narrative's synonym cluster;
- penetration = numerator / denominator;
- z-score = (current penetration − historical mean) / historical standard deviation, with the current month excluded from the baseline.

Retain corpus/query version, date basis, raw counts and baseline window. A zero denominator or zero/insufficient historical variance makes the relevant statistic unavailable. Disclose changes in corpus coverage and thin samples. Attention is not sentiment, truth, investor consensus or causal price impact. State unavailable source coverage rather than implying access to an unconnected proprietary feed.
