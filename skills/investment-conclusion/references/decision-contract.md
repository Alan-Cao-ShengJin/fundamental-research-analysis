# Stock decision and communication contract

Use for a completed single-stock recommendation and its user-facing brief. Narrow factual, historical or governance-only work need not manufacture a recommendation. Keep incomplete research explicitly incomplete; do not invent a target to satisfy this format.

## The decision block

Lead with one **Buy**, **Hold** or **Sell** call. Apply the return rules below. Do not substitute Neutral, Watch, Trim, Avoid, a valuation range or confidence wording for the primary label. Confidence and conditions follow the call. Sell means unattractive to buy and reduce/exit if owned, subject to a separate portfolio assessment; it does not authorise a short sale.

### Return hurdle and calls

Calculate base-case price return as `primary_target / reference_price - 1`, on the same currency and share basis, before rounding. Keep the **rating** separate from **investment attractiveness**. A Buy label means the target offers more than 10% price upside; it does not by itself mean the opportunity is worth allocating capital to.

| Base-case price return | Rating | Investment attractiveness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Below -10% | Sell | Explain the loss risk and reasons to avoid buying |
| -10% to +10%, inclusive | Hold | Limited upside/downside to the selected target |
| Above +10% but below +50% | Buy | Unattractive under this mandate's return hurdle |
| +50% or more | Buy | Meets the minimum upside hurdle; assess risk before calling it attractive |

Exactly -10% and +10% are Hold; +50% does not change the Buy label. This mandate normally requires at least 50% base-case price upside to describe an investment as attractive, over the stated target horizon, normally twelve months. This is the user's selection hurdle, not a universal investing rule. A current explicit mandate can change its basis or level; record that choice.

For example, a supported target offering +20% is **Buy, but unattractive**: the price can rise, but the reward falls short of the user's requirement. Do not change it to Hold or Sell merely because it fails the 50% attractiveness hurdle. Explain costs, opportunity cost, time and downside separately. A Buy label does not itself recommend a portfolio allocation.

Use the primary target, not a bull target or a more generous alternative method, to test attractiveness. Keep prospective dividends separate. Do not silently substitute gross total return, probability-weighted return or an annualised number. Meeting 50% upside alone does not establish an attractive expected return: the target must be credible and losses, governance, costs and timing acceptable. Do not invent costs or precise probabilities where evidence is missing.

Do not raise the target, assume a higher exit P/E or extend the horizon to manufacture 50% upside. Keep the supported target and show an entry price meeting the attractiveness hurdle when useful: `maximum entry price = primary target / 1.50`, before costs. Distinguish the price-target rating from whether the investment offers enough reward for new capital.

Hold still requires a target/date, signed price return, bear/bull cases, thesis and what would change the view. Missing essentials remain incomplete research, not Hold. Recalculate the rating and attractiveness when the target, reference price or mandate changes. If a risk invalidates the central target, revise the valuation and dependent rating; do not conceal it behind a separate attractiveness label.

| Field | Required meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Security and reference | Issuer, exchange/ticker, exact share class, reporting currency, reference share price and quote timestamp/status |
| Call | Buy, Hold or Sell from the primary target's price return under the rules above |
| Attractiveness | Separate judgment; state when a Buy is unattractive because upside is below 50%, and assess risk even when that hurdle is met |
| Key thesis sentence | One sentence immediately beside/below the call explaining the pivotal reason and economic consequence |
| Primary target price | One selected, sourced/model-linked price for an explicit target date/horizon; state why the primary method is preferred |
| Return to target | `(target_price / reference_price - 1) * 100`; signed percentage, same currency/share basis |
| Downside case | Bear target at the same horizon, its signed price return and the few assumptions that cause the loss |
| Upside case | Bull target at the same horizon, its signed price return and the few assumptions needed |
| Distributions and total return | Prospective dividends/buybacks treated consistently; show dividend contribution separately and total/annualised return when useful. Do not add past paid dividends or count buybacks twice |
| What changes the call | Evidence/catalyst and time horizon, plus the principal invalidation condition |

The headline target is the analyst's attainable market-price scenario at the stated horizon. Intrinsic value today, a margin-of-safety entry level, an optimistic exit-multiple case and a published broker target are different quantities. Label them separately; roll forward a present-value estimate before calling it a future price target. A range can supplement the primary target, not replace it. If valuation methods disagree materially, explain the selection rather than averaging them to achieve a preferred call.

Choose the primary valuation by the company's economics and examine relevant peers and its own historical forward valuation using the [method-selection guide](../../valuation-thesis/references/method-selection.md). Show what supports any rerating/derating and how much return comes from earnings growth versus multiple change. Short or unavailable historical coverage stays explicit. The brief displays the selected target and decisive reason; detailed valuation comparisons and internal audit records stay outside it.

Match the target to the [valuation driver](../../valuation-thesis/references/valuation-driver-and-dividends.md). A dividend-led recommendation must quantify payout, sustainable annual DPS/yield, recurring cash coverage and room for more payout. Explain the selected required yield and distinguish it from evidence for a higher peer P/E. Annual forward yield and cash distributions receivable within the target horizon are different figures; the total-return calculation uses the latter.

Present the recommendation as a supported stock pitch using the [visual pitch format](../../research-brief/references/visual-reports.md) and [natural-writing edit](../../research-brief/references/natural-writing.md). The one-page brief captures its gist; the fuller visual pitch develops the business, evidence, forecasts, governance, valuation, return path and counter-case. The appendix cannot be the only place a headline number or key argument is explained.

Apply the [valuation calendar](../../valuation-thesis/references/valuation-calendar.md). Record the current fiscal year, selected forward earnings year and target date separately. Keep the headline P/E and target/scenarios on that selected forward period; explain exceptions rather than quietly rolling another year. Round headline multiples naturally, with exact fiscal-year/profit/share/FX arithmetic visible nearby.

Use the same horizon and quotation basis across base/bear/bull. Scenario targets are not guaranteed prices or expected returns. For Sell, the bull return is the rally/forgone-upside risk, not a short-position loss calculation. Keep the target evidence-based; neither the desired rating nor attractiveness judgment can justify changing it.

## Explain the economics to an informed nonspecialist

Use compact connected paragraphs, defining company-specific terms on first use. Assume the reader understands revenue, profit and valuation, but not the issuer's specialised products or acronyms.

**Business model:** who pays, for what, how revenue is earned, the main costs/reinvestment and how cash can reach owners. Distinguish customer money/assets from company revenue, cash flow and shareholder claims. Explain the core engine before secondary initiatives.

**Competitive edge and moat:** why customers choose the business and why rivals cannot readily remove its pricing power or returns. Identify the mechanism, evidence, durability and principal erosion risk. Growth, market size, a licence, brand recognition or an AI strategy alone does not prove a moat. It is acceptable to conclude that the advantage is limited or no durable moat is demonstrated.

**Why Buy, Hold or Sell:** state the pivotal reason directly. For Hold, explain why the target is close to the price. For Buy, distinguish the potential price gain from whether it is attractive enough to invest. Identify the evidence or entry price that would change the judgment. Distinguish the company's competitive advantage from the investment insight. Explain what today's price appears to assume, why the evidence differs, how earnings or cash change, and how shareholders earn a return.

Do not assert that the whole market has missed a fact without evidence. Use conditional price-implied expectations or attributed views when broad consensus is unavailable. If no overlooked driver is established, say so and explain the call through valuation, required return or persistent governance constraints. A popular narrative or a known fact is not a research edge by itself.

**Governance and realisation:** explain whether minority holders can receive the business's value, why a discount could persist, and what credible event or cash-return route could overcome it. Business improvement alone does not guarantee repricing. Cash distributions and per-share compounding can support a Buy without multiple expansion when their economics and shareholder access are supported.

If a higher valuation drives the target, apply the [rerating test](../../valuation-thesis/references/method-selection.md#rerating-and-derating). State why investors discount the shares today, what changes, which evidence will demonstrate it and why that supports the selected P/E by the target date. An earnings date is an opportunity to show evidence, not itself a reason for a higher valuation. Explain the no-rerating case as "if investors still pay today's P/E for the forecast earnings" and show the price/return. Do not use unexplained shorthand such as unchanged multiple.

## Machine-consumable result

Retain a referenced decision record containing the call; separate attractiveness judgment, hurdle, basis and whether met; security/quote basis; target date and primary/bear/bull prices; calculated price returns; separately defined distributions/total returns; business-model/moat summary; pivotal insight and evidence; governance consequences; catalyst/invalidation; model/engine/run and market-data versions; method selection and material uncertainty. Missing essential values stay null with a reason and the research stays incomplete.

Use the existing strict handoff envelope to reference the record and its claims/calculations. Do not insert undocumented fields into that envelope. On an upstream revision, mark the prior target/call stale and recompute dependent figures before using them in a report.
