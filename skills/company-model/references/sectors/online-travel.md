# Online travel agencies

Use with the [sector guide](index.md). The card is a guide: work out how this company
actually makes money before using it, and take each business unit separately. Skip the
rows that do not apply.

## 1. What is sold, to whom, and who decides demand

Online travel agencies sell hotel rooms, air and rail tickets, package tours and
attraction tickets through their apps. They earn in three ways.

- **Commission** paid by the hotel or airline when the stay or trip is completed.
- **A margin**, where the agency collects from the traveller and pays the supplier
  later.
- **Advertising** and referral fees.

Hotels pay the highest rates. Air and rail tickets pay little. Package tours are
booked at their full price where the agency runs the tour, and as a fee where it only
sells it.

Demand is what people spend on travel, times the share booked online, times the
agency's share. Rivals include the hotels' and airlines' own apps, and the large
shopping and video apps that sell travel. Agencies buy much of their traffic with
advertising, most of all outside their home market.

Numbers that get confused:

- **Bookings** against **revenue**.
- Revenue counted when the trip is **booked** against when it is **completed**.
- The **rate earned** on each product, and revenue stated before or after coupons.
- **Room nights** against the **room rate**.
- Profit **with** and **without** share-based pay.
- Operating profit against gains on **investments** and interest on cash.

Signs the card does not fit: the company owns the hotels or the aircraft (see the
[hotels card](hotels.md) and the [airlines card](airlines.md)); it is a general shopping
platform (see the [e-commerce and advertising card](ecommerce-advertising-platforms.md));
or it runs tours with its own coaches, guides and hotels.

## 3. The data behind the rows

- **Company.** Revenue by product each quarter, with comments on growth in hotel
  bookings, travel abroad and the international business. Some give bookings and room
  nights each quarter, and others only once a year.
- **Industry:**

| Source | What exists |
| --- | --- |
| Travel | Trips and spending over each public holiday (the tourism ministry). Monthly air passengers and seats scheduled. Rail passengers |
| Hotels | Occupancy and rates by city (STR) |
| Peers | Other agencies' bookings, room nights and marketing |
| App users | Monthly app users from third parties |
| Rules | Visa changes. Competition rulings on what agencies may ask of hotels |

- **What leads revenue.** Flights scheduled. Visa changes. Bookings ahead of each
  holiday, which the agencies publish. Hotel room rates.
- **Backtest.** Bookings times the rate earned against revenue by product.

## 4. Costs and margin

- Gross margin is high. Marketing is the large cost and the one management can change.
- The leader in its home market earns a high margin. Expansion abroad is paid for with
  marketing.
- Hotels earn far more than tickets, so the mix of products moves the margin.

## 5. Capacity, capex and working capital

- The business needs little capex.
- It collects from travellers before it pays suppliers, so growth brings cash in.
- Most agencies hold large cash balances and stakes in other companies.

## 6. Cycle, policy and traps

- Travel is among the first things cut in a downturn and among the fastest to return.
- Regulators can limit commissions and the terms agencies set for hotels.
- The summer quarter is the peak.
- Traps:
  - bookings growth read as revenue growth while rates earned fall;
  - more room nights at lower room rates;
  - growth abroad bought with marketing that does not repay;
  - profit quoted without share-based pay;
  - investment gains inside profit;
  - coupons moved between revenue and cost.

## 7. What investors argue about, and the yardstick

- How fast travel abroad and the international business grow.
- How much share video and shopping apps take.
- Whether the rates earned hold.
- What is done with the cash.

Yardstick: P/E, with EV/EBITDA beside it
([yardstick list](../../../valuation-thesis/references/yardsticks.md)).

## Worked build

Travellers book 1,000 of hotel stays and 2,000 of air tickets. The agency earns 9% on
hotels and 2% on tickets: 90 plus 40, which is 130.

The next year hotel bookings grow 15% at the same rate, and ticket bookings grow 5%
while airlines cut the commission to 1.5%. Revenue is 103.5 plus 31.5, which is 135, up
4% on bookings up 8%.

Of that revenue, 100 comes from home and 35 from abroad. Marketing is 20 at home and 28
abroad: a fifth of revenue in one and four-fifths in the other.

All figures are invented. They show the relationships, not a forecast.
