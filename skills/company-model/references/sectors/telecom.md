# Telecom operators and network infrastructure

Apply the [shared sector guide](index.md) when selecting the activity and building
its forecast.

## Choose the relevant activity

Separate mobile, fixed broadband, enterprise/wholesale, equipment resale, towers and
satellite assets by relevant market.

## Operating build

Roll subscribers through gross additions, churn, acquisitions and migrations. Revenue
uses average active subscribers × average monthly revenue per user (ARPU) × months in
the forecast period, or another explicitly matched billing interval.
Premises reached by the broadband network × take-up rate produces customers, not an
extra multiplier after using subscriber counts.

Separate handset from service revenue. Towers use billable sites × tenants per site ×
rent, respecting lease and ownership terms.

Link network utilisation and quality to maintenance/expansion investment.

## Evidence and calibration

Gather net additions, churn and customer mix, ARPU definitions, promotion expiry,
competition, spectrum payment/renewal schedules, capitalised costs and capex cycles.
Reconcile cash rent, lease liability, earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and
amortisation (EBITDA), and the issuer's EBITDA-after-leases measure, interest and tax
consistently.

Verify coverage/network-quality claims and actual fibre or spectrum capacity use.

## Checks and valuation

Do not capitalise a temporary capex trough or ignore spectrum cash payments. If cash
leases are already deducted from EBITDA-after-leases, do not deduct them again.

Dividend coverage = comparable cash available / dividends; below 1 indicates a gap, not
the reverse ratio. Match debt, leases and spectrum obligations once in enterprise/cash
valuation; sale-leaseback proceeds do not remove the operating burden.

## Worked example

Service revenue of 126,000 less operating cash costs of 80,000, network investment
15,000, spectrum 10,000, leases 5,000, interest 2,000 and tax 3,000 leaves 11,000.
Dividends of 10,000 are covered 1.1 times.

Cash leases are deducted once.

All figures are invented. They illustrate selected relationships, not an issuer
forecast or a complete financial model.
