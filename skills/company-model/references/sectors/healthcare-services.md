# Healthcare services, devices and research outsourcing

Apply the [shared sector guide](index.md) when selecting the activity and building
its forecast.

## Choose the relevant activity

Choose care delivery, devices/consumables, diagnostics, contract research organisations
(CROs) or contract development and manufacturing organisations (CDMOs). Scientific
efficacy and provider/manufacturer economics are different questions.

## Operating build

Care revenue = reimbursable procedures/visits × net realised payment by payer/service,
constrained by staffing and usable capacity. Device sales separate new units × net
average selling price (ASP) from active installed base × procedure usage × consumable
revenue per procedure.

CRO/CDMO backlog bridges signed executable scope, cancellations, additions and
recognition, subject to customer funding and project capacity.

## Evidence and calibration

Gather reimbursement schedules and payer mix, reimbursement denials and collection
delays, wages and agency-staffing costs, clinical quality/compliance and utilisation.
Devices need active versus shipped installs, procedure volumes and service/consumable
attachment.

Research outsourcing needs order definitions, termination clauses, funding/customer
concentration, validation timelines and actual execution resources.

## Checks and valuation

One-time procedure catch-up or inventory replenishment is not permanent demand growth.
Higher device installs can coexist with low utilisation.

Separate organic growth, acquisitions, amortisation and recurring compliance
expenditure. Value supported cash flows and returns on capital; use pharma
evidence/probability methods only for material clinical development exposure.

## Worked example

100 opening installations plus 20 placements less 10 retirements gives 110 closing
installations. Assuming an evenly timed change, 105 average active devices × 100 annual
procedures × 5 consumable revenue per procedure gives 52,500 of consumable revenue.

All figures are invented. They illustrate selected relationships, not an issuer
forecast or a complete financial model.
