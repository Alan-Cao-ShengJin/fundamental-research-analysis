# Semiconductors and their value chain

Apply the [shared sector guide](index.md) when selecting the activity and building
its forecast.

## Choose the relevant activity

Identify fabless design, foundry or integrated device manufacturer (IDM), memory,
equipment, materials, packaging, or intellectual-property (IP) licensing. Different
stages require different units and capital models.

## Operating build

For chip demand use end-system units × chips per system × supported supplier share ×
average selling price (ASP). For fabrication use nameplate wafer capacity × utilisation
= actual starts, then dies per wafer and yield where relevant to sellable output.

Actual starts already reflect utilisation. Memory needs bit shipments and bit pricing
separately.

Equipment uses customer capital expenditure, tool intensity, orders/acceptance and
installed-base services. Intellectual-property licensing and shipment royalties use
different recognition paths.

## Evidence and calibration

Gather issuer product/node/end-market sales, yields and ramps where disclosed, capacity
additions, customer capital expenditure, channel/customer/producer inventories,
design-win-to-production dates, purchase commitments and prepayments. World
Semiconductor Trade Statistics (WSTS), the Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA) and
SEMI can help locate industry data.

Verify each series, its coverage and access terms before use. Export controls require
current official rules, product/customer applicability and dates.

## Checks and valuation

Distinguish restocking from end demand and scarce-supply pricing from durable returns.
Link utilisation/yield to absorption, depreciation and startup costs.

A design win is not a shipped sale. Trace committed supply when demand falls.

Use through-cycle cash/earnings and supported technology returns; do not capitalise peak
pricing or treat permanent market-access loss only as a discount-rate premium.

## Worked example

Capacity of 1,000 wafers at 80% utilisation gives 800 wafer starts. At 100 dies per
wafer and 90% yield, output is 72,000 good dies.

Selling 70,000 with 1,000 in opening stock leaves 3,000 in closing stock.

All figures are invented. They illustrate selected relationships, not an issuer
forecast or a complete financial model.
