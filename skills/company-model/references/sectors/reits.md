# Property owners, developers and mortgage REITs

Apply the [shared sector guide](index.md) when selecting the activity and building
its forecast.

## Choose the relevant activity

Distinguish property portfolios earning rent, developers and mortgage-funded investments.
Real estate investment trusts (REITs) can hold different types of exposure; their cash,
risk and accounting models depend on the underlying assets and funding.

## Operating build

Property rent uses lettable area × occupied share × net effective rent over the period,
less property operating costs, giving net operating income (NOI). Keep a consistent
same-property pool; acquisitions, disposals and developments are separate.

Net asset value (NAV) capitalises compatible forward NOI by appropriate capitalisation
rates matched to each asset and location, then adjusts claims once. Reconcile reported
profit to [Nareit-defined funds from operations (FFO)](https://www.reit.com/glossary/funds-operation-ffo), where applicable, and issuer-defined adjusted funds from operations (AFFO) including recurring leasing/maintenance cash and noncash rent effects.

## Evidence and calibration

Obtain occupancy, lease expiries, renewal/new-lease spreads, incentives, tenant
improvements, bad debts and development completion/yield. Gather fixed/floating debt
maturities, hedges, covenant definitions, issuance and distribution restrictions.

Developers need contracted presales, delivery recognition, construction payments,
restricted funds and guarantees. Mortgage REITs need asset/funding spreads, haircuts and
duration/prepayment risk.

## Checks and valuation

Rising occupancy can be purchased through rent-free periods and incentives. AFFO is not
standardised; justify each adjustment and share-based compensation treatment without
duplicating dilution.

Test refinancing and cap-rate stress together. NAV discount or an acquisition increasing
earnings per share alone does not prove value creation or destruction; model funding
terms and per-share economics.

Cross-check equity REITs using P/AFFO and implied cap rates on consistently defined NOI and enterprise value. Mortgage REITs instead need P/book value against sustainable spread returns and asset/funding risks.

## Worked example

A property generating 12,000 of forward NOI at a 6% capitalisation rate has a gross
value of 200,000. Subtract 100,000 debt and add 10,000 owned cash to obtain 110,000
equity NAV.

AFFO of 5,200 covers dividends of 4,000 by 1.3 times.

All figures are invented. They illustrate selected relationships, not an issuer
forecast or a complete financial model.
