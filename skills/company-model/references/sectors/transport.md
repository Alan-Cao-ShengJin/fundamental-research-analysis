# Airlines, shipping, rail, airports and logistics

Apply the [shared sector guide](index.md) when selecting the activity and building
its forecast.

## Choose the relevant activity

Choose the actual operating unit and capacity convention. Fleet ownership, chartering,
leases and forwarding are different capital models.

## Operating build

Airline passenger revenue = available seat kilometres (ASK) × load factor × passenger
yield per revenue passenger kilometre (RPK). If using passenger revenue per available
seat kilometre (RASK), revenue = ASK × RASK; load is already embedded.

Shipping uses earning vessel-days × time-charter-equivalent rate on a compatible net
voyage-cost basis. Rail/freight/logistics use shipped tonnes, tonne-km or parcels ×
matching net rate.

Airports separate regulated aeronautical revenue and passenger-linked commercial income.

## Evidence and calibration

Gather network/product mix, load/yield/unit costs, fleet deliveries/retirements, active
days, industry orderbook, ports/slots, fuel and foreign-exchange hedges, leases and
fixed purchase commitments. Reconcile maintenance, heavy checks/drydock cash and
capitalisation.

Distinguish spot/contract exposures and forwarder gross pass-through from economic
margin.

## Checks and valuation

Higher load or parcels can conceal price deterioration. The time-charter-equivalent rate
(TCE) already deducts certain voyage costs; avoid counting them again.

Lease accounting may already put obligations on balance sheet; do not recapitalise them
twice. Use supply-aware cycle cash, fleet or asset net asset value and consistent
lease-adjusted peers; fixed-capacity commitments must survive low-rate cases.

## Worked example

100 million ASK at an 80% load factor produces 80 million RPK. A yield of 0.10 per RPK
gives 8 million of passenger revenue.

The equivalent passenger RASK is 0.08; multiplying 100 million ASK by 0.08 gives the
same revenue, without another load-factor adjustment.

All figures are invented. They illustrate selected relationships, not an issuer
forecast or a complete financial model.
