# Vehicles, components and automotive finance

Apply the [shared sector guide](index.md) when selecting the activity and building
its forecast.

## Choose the relevant activity

Separate vehicle manufacturing, suppliers, batteries, service/software and the captive
financing business. Analyse finance separately before reconciling the consolidated
group.

## Operating build

Vehicle sales use model/region wholesale and retail units, channel stock and net
realised average selling price (ASP). Contribution per vehicle = net price − genuinely
variable materials, labour, logistics and warranty; breakeven volume = fixed costs /
positive unit contribution.

Full gross margin includes fixed absorption, so do not use it again as the contribution
denominator. Supplier revenue can use customer-platform units × content per unit ×
awarded share with launch timing and price reductions.

## Evidence and calibration

Gather retail registrations versus deliveries, refundable orders, discounts, model mix,
production capacity/ramp, battery capacity and cost per kilowatt-hour (kWh) and
commodity pass-through lags. Separate regulatory credits and ancillary profit.

Obtain warranty/recall/residual-value assumptions, capitalised development, supplier
finance, industrial cash and credit/funding/capital schedules for the captive financing
business.

## Checks and valuation

New-factory ramp and launch spending may recur. Test utilisation, pricing, unit costs,
cash runway and financing together; refundable orders are not delivered demand.

Software adoption and autonomy economics need their own evidence and risk cases. Use
through-cycle earnings, cash-flow or sum-of-the-parts valuation matched to manufacturing
and finance; do not impose a universal method prohibition merely from the sector label.

## Worked example

A net vehicle price of 20 less variable unit cost of 15 gives contribution of 5 per
vehicle. Fixed costs of 400 require 80 vehicles to break even.

Selling 100 gives 100 of manufacturing contribution after those fixed costs.
