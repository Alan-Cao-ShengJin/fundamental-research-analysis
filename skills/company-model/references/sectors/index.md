# Sector research and modelling guide

Use these references to select the operating drivers, evidence and accounting checks
that matter for the company. They support the existing research sequence from
observations through assumptions and component forecasts to financial results.

## Cards are a guide

A card is guidance for the build, not a rule to follow strictly.

- **Take each business unit separately.** One company can hold units that are completely
  different. Each unit gets its own card, or the no-card route, and its own build.
- **Check the fit before using a card.** A company can match a card's description and
  still run a different business model. Work out how this company actually makes money
  first. Where that differs from the card, model what the company actually does.
- **Fall back when the conventional build would mislead.** If the business is unusual
  enough that it cannot be modelled as a conventional company of its kind, use
  [when no card fits](#when-no-card-fits) for that unit and say why.

## Select by the source of value and risk

Identify the activities generating material earnings, invested capital, cash or
future value. Choose the relevant reference by its economic mechanism, not the
company's label or stock-market classification. Use additional references only
for material activities requiring different drivers, accounting or valuation.
There is no fixed maximum: a diversified company may need several distinct
builds, but should not inherit every KPI mentioned across them.

An equipment supplier to mines uses industrial/equipment economics; owning mines
uses extraction economics. A battery factory is manufacturing, whereas an owned
power project and an EPC contract need different recognition and funding builds.
An online lender may need credit and capital analysis despite a software interface.
A hospital does not require drug-pipeline success probabilities unless it actually
owns a material development exposure. If no reference fits, follow
[when no card fits](#when-no-card-fits).

## When no card fits

Use this for a business unit with no card, and for one whose card matches the label but
not the way the unit actually makes money. Do the card's work for that unit before
building. From the company's filings and its two or three closest peers, write down in
the study:

1. What it sells, to whom, and who decides demand.
2. The driver tree, as the rows the revenue build will have, from industry volume down
   to company units and price.
3. The data behind each row: what the company discloses, which industry series exist
   and how often, and what leads reported revenue and by how long. Backtest the
   candidates against reported results.
4. Costs and margin: the cost lines, which of them are fixed, and how input prices pass
   through.
5. Capacity, capex and working capital particular to the business.
6. The cycle, the rules that matter and the definitions that mislead.
7. What investors argue about, and the yardstick from the
   [yardstick list](../../../valuation-thesis/references/yardsticks.md).

A nearby card can supply some of these parts. Say which, and what it does not cover.
Do not force an unrelated card onto the business.

## Use the references to construct assumptions

Build the estimates with the [foundational-build method](../foundational-builds.md).
Map each operating metric to its exact period, units, perimeter, stock/flow,
average/closing and gross/net definitions; provider or broker terminology is not
automatically the issuer's. Explain the latest observed profit or loss before
extrapolating a new margin. Check each formula's ratios, units and double counting
for the task.

The sector references supply methods, not forecast inputs. Establish any claimed
normal margin, cycle average, success probability or peer range from dated evidence.
Their worked examples cannot supply those empirical assumptions.

Accounting, tax, tariff, reimbursement, regulatory capital, product approvals and
licence rules vary by place and date. Check the current primary source for an
actual assignment. The references describe methods, not a current rules database.
Issuer filings/supplements are the usual starting point; regulator datasets,
technical reports, contracts and matched industry series then fill specific gaps,
retrieved through [evidence intake](../../../evidence-intake/SKILL.md).
