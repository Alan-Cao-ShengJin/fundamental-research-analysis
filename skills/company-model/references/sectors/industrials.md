# Machinery, engineered projects and aftermarket

Apply the [shared sector guide](index.md) when selecting the activity and building
its forecast.

## Choose the relevant activity

Separate short-cycle products, long-cycle equipment, contracted projects, defence
programmes and installed-base services.

## Operating build

Short-cycle sales connect end demand and distributor inventory to shipments/net prices.
Long-cycle backlog = opening + executable orders − recognised deliveries − cancellations
± identified scope and foreign-exchange changes.

Recognise project revenue using the applicable performance or acceptance method,
milestones and costs; separate cash billing/collection. Services use active installed
equipment, usage, attachment, price and retirement, rather than duplicating the machine
sales base.

## Evidence and calibration

Gather order firmness, options, cancel rights, project type/customer funding, scheduled
delivery, advances, contract assets, cost-to-complete and groups of contracts with
different expected margins. Distinguish equipment, spares, service and geography mix.

Customer capital expenditure and peers provide demand checks, not automatic issuer
sales. Obtain materials/labour pricing lags and staffing/capacity for growth.

## Checks and valuation

Backlog is not cash or guaranteed revenue. Audit profit recognition against collections
and cost revisions.

A historic service growth rate is not a fixed ceiling: installed-base age, attachment,
usage and pricing can change. Value supported mid-cycle returns and, where useful,
equipment, projects and aftermarket activities separately, with funded reinvestment and
acquisition costs.

## Worked example

Opening backlog of 100 plus 50 executable orders less 80 recognised deliveries and 10
cancellations closes at 60. A separate active installed base of 100 machines with 50%
service adoption and service revenue of 0.2 per machine adds 10 of revenue.

All figures are invented. They illustrate selected relationships, not an issuer
forecast or a complete financial model.
