# Investor judgment under imperfect information

Use this reference when choosing evidence, interpreting interested sources, setting assumptions and reaching a company call. Investment decisions normally precede complete information. The objective is a reasoned view of prospective return, downside and what the price already assumes, with enough evidence to distinguish competing explanations. Do not make certainty or success in every plausible scenario a condition for a recommendation.

Integrate the relevant dimensions using the [materiality principle](underwriting-standard.md#integrate-the-analysis-by-materiality). Recent conversation topics do not automatically deserve more weight than earlier principles. Apply specialist methods when they help explain value, risk or repricing; the presence of a skill is not a requirement to make its topic the main thesis.

## Choose information by its decision value

Start with the economic disagreement and the few drivers most capable of changing value. Ask what observation would discriminate between the leading explanations, how much it could change the call, whether it can be obtained promptly and what it costs. Prefer evidence close to the mechanism: purchases and inventory for demand, renewal/pricing for customer economics, actual collections for cash conversion, contract terms for cost incidence.

Prioritise requests by their likely reduction in decision uncertainty, materiality and accessibility. A complete-looking dataset can add little if it measures the wrong thing; a rough but relevant observation may be more useful. This ranking can be qualitative. Do not invent information-value scores or probabilities to make the process appear quantitative.

Separate facts worth retrieving now, useful estimates that can be made from available evidence, and irreducible unknowns to carry as risk. Triangulate sparse observations with industry economics, historical base rates and appropriate analogues. Explain where judgment takes over. Stop collecting when the likely decision benefit is small relative to cost or delay; retain a targeted follow-up trigger instead of indefinitely postponing a call.

## Weigh the claim, source and incentives separately

Assess access to the underlying fact, measurement quality, specificity, timeliness, incentives and demonstrated accuracy for this claim. The same source can be useful for reported data and less persuasive about a forecast or target. A primary source establishes what was disclosed; it does not make the speaker's interpretation or future expectation objectively true.

For sell-side work, separate original observations and model mechanics from estimates, recommendation and price target. Inspect definitions, revisions, scenario choices and the evidence behind the numbers. Consider relevant disclosed commercial relationships and incentives without assuming dishonesty or applying a universal discount. Multiple notes repeating management or one channel check are not independent corroboration. Consensus helps map expectations and dispersion; it is neither truth nor an instruction to conform or be contrarian.

For management, consider incentives to influence expectations alongside operational knowledge, control over outcomes and demonstrated execution. Examine emphasis, omissions, KPI/perimeter changes and tension between words, cash commitments and actual conduct. Treat these as hypotheses to test, not proof of concealed motives. Do not use tone, confidence, nationality, personality or a single miss to infer integrity.

Apply the same discipline to bullish and bearish commentators. Commercial incentives can affect a channel source, short seller, customer or expert too. Skepticism should change the weight and uncertainty assigned to a claim when justified, rather than mechanically reject all interested sources.

## Learn management's forecasting and execution pattern

Build a dated guidance-to-delivery record using the original guidance available at each date, subsequent revisions and matched actuals. Record metric/perimeter, range, timing, operating assumptions and relevant external events. Where history permits, distinguish initial guidance from late-year guidance and examine errors by metric, direction and business regime.

Consider repeated optimistic guidance followed by cuts; conservatively framed targets followed by delivery; consistent execution; and inconsistent or insufficient history. Separate forecasting skill from operational performance: an easy target can be beaten during deteriorating economics, and a capable operator can miss because conditions changed. Check changes of management, acquisitions, accounting, currency and adjustment definitions before transferring an old pattern into a new forecast. Do not infer a stable bias from a handful of unmatched observations.

Use a demonstrated pattern to inform the base case and range alongside current operating evidence. Do not automatically extrapolate recurring beats or apply the same haircut to every management forecast. Unknown track record remains unknown, with an appropriately wider range. An incentive hurdle is not guidance, and either can differ from management's private expectation.

## Separate probability of success from the payoff at the price

The likelihood of a favourable outcome and the attractiveness of its payoff are different questions. A strong business, likely earnings beat or high historical strategy win rate can be a poor investment if the entry price already allows for success and a disappointment causes a much larger loss. Conversely, a lower-probability outcome can be attractive if prospective gains adequately compensate for losses and uncertainty. Assess the terms offered by the price as well as the quality of the underlying opportunity.

Define success before discussing its probability: business growth, meeting guidance, a positive shareholder return and beating a benchmark are different events. An earnings beat can coincide with a falling share price. Measure investment payoffs from the actual reference price on a common horizon and capital basis, including relevant distributions, FX, financing and implementation costs. Keep company-outcome probabilities distinct from probabilities of investment returns; neither analyst confidence nor a price-implied assumption is automatically a measured win probability.

When defensible scenario probabilities exist, expected net return is `sum(p_i * r_i)`, with mutually exclusive scenarios covering the relevant outcomes and weights summing to one. In a simplified two-outcome case with net gain U, net loss L (both positive magnitudes) and success probability p, expected return is `p * U - (1 - p) * L`; break-even p is `L / (U + L)`. This threshold is conditional on the assumed payoffs, not evidence that the actual probability is known. Distinguish expected return from the gain in the winning case, the base target's upside and the upside/downside ratio. The ratio alone ignores likelihood.

For illustration only, a 90% chance of gaining 5% and a 10% chance of losing 60% has an expected return of -1.5%; the break-even success probability is about 92.3%. Frequent small wins do not compensate for the assumed occasional loss. Real equities have more than two possible outcomes; this arithmetic does not establish a stock's probabilities or bound its losses.

Use qualitative likelihoods, reasoned ranges and sensitivity to alternative weights when precise probabilities lack support. Numerical expected value is optional; explicitly compare the likelihood and size of gains/losses even without it. Do not populate arbitrary scenario probabilities to obtain a desired recommendation, or treat three handpicked scenarios as a complete distribution. Check omitted tail losses and the possibility that success is already more than priced in. The primary target remains distinct from any probability-weighted valuation and retains the existing rating rule.

## Test regime dependence and the ability to bear losses

Ask which macroeconomic, liquidity and financing conditions support the apparent win rate, whether they are sustainable and what is already priced. Test a regime change that can simultaneously weaken earnings/cash flow, compress the multiple, reduce liquidity and raise funding costs. Do not extrapolate a favourable-regime sample as an unconditional probability or assume rare losses are absent because they are missing from recent history.

Evaluate severity and recoverability of loss, solvency, dilution, forced exits and portfolio concentration as well as mean return. A positive arithmetic expected return does not establish capital survival, attractive compound growth or a suitable position size. Correlated losses and constraints can make an otherwise attractive standalone payoff unsuitable for the portfolio. Do not turn a subjective probability estimate into an automatic sizing or leverage rule.

Reassess when price changes: the same business forecast can become a worse investment after a rally and a better one after a decline, subject to revised risks. Compare the opportunity with cash or an appropriate benchmark on a consistent horizon and risk basis. State whether the decision rests on favourable odds, attractive payoffs or both, and what would erode that advantage.

## Make an independent estimate and an actionable judgment

Build the base case from the most persuasive combination of economics, observed trajectory, historical experience, constraints and source credibility. Explain why it is more likely or decision-relevant than competing cases. Preserve a source's actual statement separately from the analyst's adjustment; an educated estimate is legitimate without becoming a reported fact.

Use reasonable ranges and coherent adverse cases. Probabilities can be qualitative, or numerical when a defensible basis exists; avoid precise weights without support. A credible downside scenario can reverse the sign of the return without invalidating the base-case call. Assess prospective reward, loss severity, financing survival, price paid, persistence of discounts and the margin for estimation error together. Do not require investment success in every scenario.

Reach a Buy/Hold/Sell when a defensible central estimate and return/risk judgment can be formed. State conviction, the decisive assumptions and observable conditions for changing the view. Preserve the existing inclusive +/-10% Hold rule for a completed primary target; incomplete information alone is not Hold.

Keep research incomplete only when missing essentials prevent a meaningful estimate or make the proposed call arbitrary: for example, unresolved instrument identity, an unusable financial baseline, or uncertainty about obligations/cash access so severe that economic value and survival cannot be reasonably assessed. Distinguish these from normal forecast uncertainty or a missing preferred dataset. A confident-looking target cannot repair a factual foundation that is unusable.

Apply incentive and credibility analysis within the research. Investor-facing output should state the resulting forecast judgment and evidence concisely; avoid generic commentary about brokers' or management's motives. Explain a specific credibility issue when it materially affects the decision, with attribution and appropriate qualification. Preserve factual accuracy even when simplifying the presentation.
